\begin{abstract}
Armstrong, Reiner and Rhoades conjectured in 2012 that the noncrossing
and algebraic parking spaces of an irreducible finite real reflection
group have the same character under the group and the cyclic group
generated by a Coxeter element. They computed the algebraic character,
proved this weak conjecture in the classical and rank-two types, and
reported Christian Stump's computer verification for $H_3$, $H_4$,
$F_4$ and $E_6$; types $E_7$ and $E_8$ remained open. We prove the weak conjecture in types $E_7$ and $E_8$.
Together with these earlier results, including Stump's reported
computation, this completes the weak conjecture for real reflection
groups. The number of points of the noncrossing parking space fixed by a
pair $(w,c^d)$ reduces to counting the elements of a conjugacy class that
lie in a coset $P_Xc^d$, where $P_X$ is the pointwise stabilizer of a
noncrossing flat. Machine-checkable certificates verify these counts, and
the completeness of the list of noncrossing elements, in exact
arithmetic. The result concerns the original conjecture, not its Fuss
generalization.

This work has not yet been reviewed by experts. The proofs, the computation programs, the certificate checkers and the text were produced by AI models (from OpenAI and Anthropic), working under the author's direction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
We complete the weak form of the parking-space conjecture of Drew
Armstrong, Victor Reiner and Brendon Rhoades, first posted on
April 8, 2012~\cite{ARR}. Let $W$ be an irreducible finite real
reflection group on its essential real reflection representation $V$, let $c$ be any Coxeter
element, let $h$ be its order, and put $C=\langle c\rangle$ and
$\omega=\exp(2\pi i/h)$. A noncrossing flat is the fixed space of an
element of the absolute-order interval $[1,c]$; this interval is defined
in Section~\ref{sec:definitions}. For a flat $X$, let $P_X$ be its
pointwise stabilizer in $W$. The noncrossing parking set is
\[
 \Park^{\NC}_W=\{[x,X]: X\text{ is a noncrossing flat},\ xP_X\in W/P_X\},
\]
where $[x,X]$ records the coset $xP_X$ as well as $X$; we call its
elements parking points. Its action is
$(w,c^d)[x,X]=[wxc^{-d},c^dX]$. Write
$\Fix_{\Park^{\NC}_W}(w,c^d)$ for the set of parking points fixed by
$(w,c^d)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For $W$ of type $E_7$ or $E_8$, every $w\in W$ and every integer $d$
satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \bigl|\Fix_{\Park^{\NC}_W}(w,c^d)\bigr|
       =(h+1)^{\dim_{\C}\ker(w-\omega^d I\mid V\otimes_{\R}\C)}.
\end{equation}
Here $h=18$ in type $E_7$ and $h=30$ in type $E_8$.
\end{theorem}

By~\cite[Proposition~2.15]{ARR}, the right side of~\eqref{eq:main} is
the value at $(w,c^d)$ of the character of the algebraic parking space,
so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} says that the two parking spaces have equal
$W\times C$-characters in types $E_7$ and $E_8$.
Armstrong, Reiner and Rhoades asked to ``check that the weakest version
of the Main Conjecture holds via computer calculation in the remaining
types $E_7, E_8$''~\cite[Problem~11.1]{ARR}; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} does
this, reusing their character formula. The $W$-character alone was
already known (see below). The new difficulty is the cyclic factor $C$:
a parking point over a flat $X$ can be fixed by $(w,c^d)$ only when
$c^dX=X$, so counting fixed points of $W$ alone misses this condition.
We reduce the count to conjugacy classes in $W$ and in the subgroups
generated by $P_X$ and a power of $c$, and check separately that our
list of noncrossing elements is complete.

To identify the exact conjecture, let $\Park^{\mathrm{alg}}_W$ denote
the algebraic parking space of~\cite[Section~2.5]{ARR}, described in
Section~\ref{sec:definitions}. With the hypotheses on $W$ and $C$
above, the concluding sentence of their weak Main Conjecture is:
\begin{quote}
``Then one has an isomorphism of $W\times C$-representations''
\[
 \C[\Park^{\mathrm{alg}}_W]
       \cong_{\C[W\times C]}\C[\Park^{\NC}_W].
\]
\end{quote}
This is equation~(2.7) of~\cite[Section~2.6]{ARR}. Their strong and
intermediate versions, in the same section, ask that for every, or for
some, homogeneous system of parameters $\Theta$ as in
Section~\ref{sec:definitions}, the common zero locus $V^\Theta$ of
$\theta_1-x_1,\ldots,\theta_n-x_n$, where $n=\dim_{\R}V$, ``consists of
$(h+1)^n$ distinct points, that have a $W\times C$-equivariant bijection
to the set $\Park^{\NC}_W$''. Each implies the weak version.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:all}
The weak Main Conjecture of Armstrong, Reiner and Rhoades holds for
every irreducible finite real reflection group: its algebraic and
noncrossing parking spaces are isomorphic as complex
$W\times C$-representations.
\end{corollary}

Table~\ref{tab:prior} lists the earlier cases. Brendon Rhoades
subsequently proved the intermediate conjecture in type $A$ for every
Fuss parameter and related it to the generic strong form
\cite[Theorems~1.1--1.2 and Table~1]{Rhoades}.

The new content is the cyclic factor. At Fuss parameter $1$, Christos
Athanasiadis and Victor Reiner showed that the noncrossing and
nonnesting flat counts agree in each Weyl-group orbit (case-by-case, by
computer in the exceptional types) \cite[Theorem~6.3]{AR};
Armstrong, Reiner and Rhoades deduced the corresponding $W$-set
isomorphism \cite[Section~2.4, equation~(2.3)]{ARR}. In particular, the
$W$-only comparison in types $E_7$ and $E_8$ was already known. Rhoades
proved the Fuss weak conjecture, hence the $W$-character identity, in
the infinite families~\cite[Table~3]{RhoadesFuss}, and Theo
Douvropoulos and Matthieu Josuat-Verg\`es checked the remaining
exceptional types by computer~\cite[Theorem~3.10 and proof]{DJV}, noting
that their computation is ``a finite statement about polynomials in $m$
(whereas Rhoades' conjectures [\ldots] involves a cyclic group of order
$mh$ and is therefore of a different nature)''~\cite[Section~3.2]{DJV}.
Cyclic sieving for noncrossing partitions is also known: David Bessis
and Victor Reiner prove it for irreducible well-generated complex
reflection groups under conjugation by a Coxeter element
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{BR}, and Christian Krattenthaler and Thomas
W.~M\"uller prove the exceptional-type cases of two cyclic-sieving
theorems for generalized noncrossing partitions
\cite[Theorems~2 and~33]{KM}. These cyclic actions are on noncrossing
partitions, not on the parking space. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} computes
the joint $W\times C$ character on the original noncrossing parking
space for $E_7$ and $E_8$. Further nearby work is credited in
Appendix~\ref{app:nearby}.

The conclusion is equality of complex characters and hence a
representation isomorphism. It does not supply an equivariant bijection
of the underlying sets, prove the intermediate or strong conjecture,
treat arbitrary Fuss parameter, or address $G_{34}$. The proof is
computer-assisted; Section~\ref{sec:computation} describes the
computation.

\section{Proof overview}\label{sec:overview}
The proof reduces the large parking sets to small group-theoretic
counts. The algebraic side is already known: Armstrong--Reiner--Rhoades
give its value as the right side of~\eqref{eq:main}
\cite[Proposition~2.15]{ARR}. It therefore suffices to compute every
value of the noncrossing parking character.

Fix a noncrossing flat $X$. A parking point over $X$ can be fixed by
$(w,c^d)$ only if $c^dX=X$. In that case its coset condition becomes
$x^{-1}wx\in P_Xc^d$. Consequently the contribution over $X$ is
$|C_W(w)|/|P_X|$ times the number of elements of the $W$-conjugacy
class of $w$ that lie in $P_Xc^d$, where $C_W(w)$ is the centralizer
of $w$. Flats in the same $C$-orbit make equal
contributions. If this orbit has length $\ell$, the group
$K=\langle P_X,c^\ell\rangle$ contains $P_X$ as a normal subgroup with
cyclic quotient. When $\ell$ divides $d$, the coset $P_Xc^d$ is a union of
$K$-conjugacy classes. Thus complete class lists for $W$ and these
groups $K$ suffice to compute the entire character.

There are two completeness obligations. First, the list of
noncrossing elements must be the whole absolute-order interval, not
just a collection with the expected cardinality. Starting from $c$,
closure under every reflection step that lowers reflection length
proves this by a reduced-factorization argument. Second, the supplied
class representatives must cover $W$ and each $K$. Centralizer-derived
class sizes, pairwise nonconjugacy, and their sum establish these
covers. Conjugating witnesses then determine how the $K$-classes lie in
$W$-classes, without relying on names in character-table libraries.

Two separately written programs check these certificates and
recompute all character values; both rely on the exact group algorithms
of GAP (Section~\ref{sec:computation}).

\section{Reflection and parking spaces}\label{sec:definitions}
As a small example, in type $A_1$ the group is $W=\{1,s\}$ acting on
a line, and $c=s$. There are two noncrossing flats, the whole line
$V$ and $\{0\}$. Their pointwise stabilizers are $\{1\}$ and $W$,
respectively, so the parking set has two points over $V$ and one over
$\{0\}$. The element $(1,s)$ fixes only the point over $\{0\}$;
the element $(s,s)$ fixes all three. These are the values $3^0$ and
$3^1$ in~\eqref{eq:main}.

In type $A_2$, the group is the symmetry group of an equilateral
triangle. Its five noncrossing flats are the plane, the three reflection
lines, and the origin. Figure~\ref{fig:a2} shows these flats, their
Coxeter rotation, and the sizes of their coset fibers.

Let $T$ be the set of reflections of $W$. The reflection length
$\ell_T(g)$ is the least number of factors in an expression of $g$ as
a product of elements of $T$. Absolute order is defined by
\[
 u\leq_T v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \ell_T(v)=\ell_T(u)+\ell_T(u^{-1}v),
 \qquad \NC(W,c)=[1,c]_T.
\]
For the Weyl groups computed here, Carter's formula gives
$\ell_T(g)=\rank(I-g)$ in the reflection
representation~\cite[Section~2, Lemma~2]{Carter}; see also
\cite[Section~2.1]{ARR}. The map $u\mapsto\Fix_V(u)$ is injective on
$\NC(W,c)$ by the noncrossing-flat embedding of Brady and Watt
\cite[Theorem~2]{BW}, used in~\cite[Definition~2.2]{ARR}. Its image
is the set of noncrossing flats. With $P_X$ the pointwise stabilizer,
the parking set and action in the introduction agree with
\cite[Definition~2.6 and Section~2.3]{ARR}.

Write $n=\dim_{\R}V$ and choose coordinates $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ on the
complexified reflection representation. A homogeneous system of
parameters is a sequence of $n$ homogeneous polynomials whose ideal has
a finite-dimensional quotient. For the algebraic space, choose one,
$\Theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n)$, of degree $h+1$, for which the
linear map $x_i\mapsto\theta_i$ from the dual reflection representation
is $W$-equivariant. Such a $\Theta$ exists for every irreducible real
reflection group, by Gordon's construction~\cite{Gordon}, which uses
Berest--Etingof--Ginzburg~\cite{BEG}; see~\cite[footnote~21]{ARR}
and, for later qualifications,~\cite[Sections~1.5, 2.11 and~2.12]{GG}.
Etingof shows that some such $\Theta$ cuts out $(h+1)^n$ distinct
points~\cite[Theorem~12.1]{ARR}. Only the character formula below is
used here. The algebraic parking
module is
\[
 \C[V\otimes_{\R}\C]/(\theta_1-x_1,\ldots,\theta_n-x_n),
\]
with the $W$-action on polynomials induced by its action on $V$, and
with $c$ acting on a homogeneous polynomial of degree $j$ by
$\omega^{-j}$. The ideal is $W$-stable since $\Theta-\mathrm{id}$ is
$W$-equivariant~\cite[Definition~2.10]{ARR}. Since $\omega^{-(h+1)}=\omega^{-1}$, the generators
$\theta_i-x_i$ all transform by the same scalar, so this action preserves
their ideal; see~\cite[Section~2.5, equation~(2.4) and Definition~2.10]{ARR}. Their
Propositions~2.11 and~2.15 show that its character is independent of
the permitted choice of $\Theta$ and equals
$(h+1)^{\dim_{\C}\ker(w-\omega^dI)}$ at $(w,c^d)$. This is the
only algebraic input needed in the comparison.

The finite model in type $E_n$, for $n=7,8$, uses the simple-root
Gram matrix $A$ with diagonal entries $2$ and off-diagonal entry $-1$
on the edges
\[
 (1,3),\ (3,4),\ (2,4),\ (4,5),\ldots,(n-1,n),
\]
and zero elsewhere. Let $e_i$ denote the $i$th standard coordinate row
vector. In simple-root coordinates the simple reflections
on row vectors are
\[
 s_i(v)=v-(vAe_i^{\mathsf T})e_i.
\]
Their orbits on the simple roots give the root system; permutation of
all roots is faithful because the simple roots span the representation.
The element used for the calculation is $c=s_1\cdots s_n$. All
Coxeter elements in a finite real reflection group are conjugate
\cite[Section~2.1]{ARR}, so
transport by conjugation gives the assertion for any choice of $c$.

\section{The finite counting reduction}\label{sec:reduction}
Write $\Cl_W(w)$ for the conjugacy class of $w$ and $C_W(w)$ for
its centralizer. An orbit of flats means an orbit under $C$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:count}
Choose one representative $X$ from each orbit of noncrossing flats,
write $\ell_X$ for its orbit length, and put
$K_X=\langle P_X,c^{\ell_X}\rangle$. For every $w\in W$ and integer
$d$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
 \bigl|\Fix_{\Park^{\NC}_W}(w,c^d)\bigr|
 =\sum_{\substack{X\text{ an orbit representative}\\\ell_X\mid d}}
   \frac{\ell_X\,|C_W(w)|}{|P_X|}
   \bigl|\Cl_W(w)\cap P_Xc^d\bigr|.
\end{equation}
Every intersection in the sum is a disjoint union of $K_X$-classes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A fixed parking point must lie over a flat fixed by $c^d$. For such a
flat $c^dP_Xc^{-d}=P_X$, so the coset condition below is independent of
its representative $x$. When
$c^dX=X$, equality of the cosets of $[wxc^{-d},X]$ and $[x,X]$
is equivalent to $x^{-1}wx\in P_Xc^d$. The map
$x\mapsto x^{-1}wx$ from $W$ to $\Cl_W(w)$ has constant fiber size
$|C_W(w)|$. Dividing by the $|P_X|$ representatives of each coset
gives
\[
 \frac{|C_W(w)|}{|P_X|}|\Cl_W(w)\cap P_Xc^d|
\]
fixed parking points over $X$.

The flat is fixed by $c^d$ exactly when $\ell_X$ divides $d$.
Conjugation by powers of $c$ carries $P_X$ to the stabilizers of the
other flats in its orbit, preserves $\Cl_W(w)$, and preserves $c^d$.
Thus all $\ell_X$ flats make the same contribution. This proves
\eqref{eq:count}.

The element $c^{\ell_X}$ normalizes $P_X$, since it preserves $X$.
Hence $P_X$ is normal in $K_X$ and $K_X/P_X$ is cyclic. If
$\ell_X\mid d$, then $c^d\in K_X$. Every coset of $P_X$ in $K_X$
is invariant under conjugation in $K_X$, because that quotient is
abelian. The intersection of such a coset with a $W$-class is therefore
a union of whole $K_X$-classes.
\end{proof}

The pointwise stabilizer $P_X$ can be constructed without recognizing
its parabolic type: it is generated by all root reflections whose
hyperplanes contain $X$. This is Steinberg's stabilizer result~\cite{Steinberg1968}, quoted by
Carter~\cite[Section~2, Lemma~1]{Carter}: an element fixing a set of
vectors is a product of reflections each fixing that set. A reflection
fixes $X$ pointwise precisely when its hyperplane contains $X$, so the
construction of $P_X$ uses all and only the required reflections.

\begin{lemma}[Complete interval cover]\label{lem:cover}
Let $S$ be a subset of $[1,c]_T$ containing $c$. Suppose that, for
every $g\in S$ and reflection $t\in T$ with
$\ell_T(gt)=\ell_T(g)-1$, the element $gt$ belongs to $S$. Then
$S=[1,c]_T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $u\leq_T c$, concatenate reduced reflection factorizations of
$u$ and $u^{-1}c$. Their concatenation is a reduced factorization
of $c$. Deleting its rightmost factors until the prefix for $u$
remains gives a descending path from $c$ to $u$, each step a right
reflection multiplication lowering length by one. Every prefix lies
in $[1,c]_T$. The closure assumption forces this entire path, including
$u$, into $S$.
\end{proof}

By Carter's formula, all lengths in this lemma and interval membership
are checked by exact ranks. For example, membership is the equality
\[
 \rank(I-g)+\rank(I-g^{-1}c)=n.
\]
The expected generalized Catalan cardinality is an additional
crosscheck; it does not replace Lemma~\ref{lem:cover}.

Appendix~\ref{app:conventions} checks that the right-action
conventions of the computation give the same counts as the left action
in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Computational verification}\label{sec:computation}
\subsection{What the certificates contain}
For each type, the certificates give the group and character data, the
noncrossing interval, its cyclic orbits of flats, and conjugacy-class
data for the groups $K_X$. They and the programs are archived at
\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23605084}.

The interval is encoded by parent edges. Its first element is $c$;
each later element is obtained from an earlier one by a specified root
reflection. A checker reconstructs these elements, checks their
distinctness and interval membership, and checks every descending
reflection step in Lemma~\ref{lem:cover}. It also computes the fixed
spaces by rational row reduction, checks that they are distinct, and
checks that the specified cyclic orbits partition them.

For $W$ and each $K_X$, the class lists are checked by group
membership, exact centralizer-derived class sizes, and pairwise
nonconjugacy. Their sizes must sum to the order of the group. These
conditions prove that the lists cover the entire group. Every supplied
representative of a $K_X$-class has a conjugating element in $W$ that
identifies its $W$-class. The checkers verify this element; they do not
infer fusion from a class name, its order, or its eigenvalues.

For each exponent $d$, membership in $P_Xc^d$ determines which
$K_X$-classes contribute. Their sizes, with the factors in
Proposition~\ref{prop:count}, give the parking character. Independently,
exact nullspaces over cyclotomic fields give the eigenvalue
multiplicities. The checkers compare the two integers for each pair
consisting of a $W$-class and an exponent $0\leq d<h$.

\begin{proposition}[Finite computation]\label{prop:finite}
For the standard root representations of $E_7$ and $E_8$, the
certificates pass both full checkers. In every $W$-conjugacy class and
for every exponent $0\leq d<h$, formula~\eqref{eq:count} equals the
right side of~\eqref{eq:main}. There are no unresolved entries.
\end{proposition}

The $E_6$ case, already known, is included as a test case; it is not a
new case. The row of $K_X$-classes is the total over the groups $K_X$
attached to the orbit representatives.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} and the checked class covers make the
calculation of Proposition~\ref{prop:count} exhaustive.
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite} establishes~\eqref{eq:main} on every
$W$-class for $0\leq d<h$. Both sides are constant on $W$-classes
and periodic in $d$ modulo $h$, so this proves all the asserted
equalities. Appendix~\ref{app:conventions} identifies the computed
action with the specified reflection representation and parking action.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:all}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} supplies the two exceptional types absent
from Table~\ref{tab:prior}. The classification of irreducible finite
real reflection groups makes this list exhaustive. For $H_3$, $H_4$
and $F_4$, the input is Christian Stump's Sage computation as reported
in~\cite[Section~2.6, the paragraph after Proposition~2.15]{ARR}; its
code and output are not part of~\cite{ARR}, so these three cases rest
on that reported computation. Of the types in that row, only $E_6$ is
recomputed here (Table~\ref{tab:computation}). Proposition~2.15
of~\cite{ARR} identifies the right side of~\eqref{eq:main} with the
algebraic parking character. Equality of complex characters of a
finite group is equivalent to isomorphism of the corresponding
complex representations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reproducing the check}
The accompanying README gives installation instructions and the single
command that checks the certificates for $E_6$, $E_7$ and $E_8$ with
both programs; it takes about five minutes. The computations used GAP
4.15.1 \cite{GAP}, with SymPy 1.14.0 \cite{SymPy} for the
rational-polynomial checks. The command also checks the exact
eigenvalue data and, as an additional crosscheck, compares the cyclic
fixed-flat counts with root-of-unity evaluations of the $q$-Catalan
polynomial of Bessis--Reiner \cite[Theorem~1.1]{BR}. The computation
uses no floating-point tests and no character-table names.

\subsection{What the proof relies on}
The proof uses ordinary reflection-group mathematics: the standard
$E_n$ root representation, Carter's reflection-length formula,
Brady--Watt's noncrossing-flat embedding, the real parabolic stabilizer
theorem, the previously established types in Table~\ref{tab:prior},
the algebraic character formula of Armstrong--Reiner--Rhoades, and
finite-group character theory. The counting and cover arguments in
Section~\ref{sec:reduction} are written mathematical arguments.

The computation relies on GAP's exact integer, rational and cyclotomic
arithmetic; its permutation multiplication, membership and group-order
algorithms; its centralizers and conjugacy tests; and its exact rank,
nullspace and row-reduction routines. The two programs implement the
certificate checks separately but share GAP's kernel, so they are not
independent implementations of it. The additional spectral checks rely
on SymPy's exact rational polynomial arithmetic. The class lists and
conjugating elements supplied with the certificates are checked by the
class covers and conjugacy tests described above.

\section{Further nearby work}\label{app:nearby}
Pavel Galashin, Thomas Lam, Minh-T\^am Quang Trinh and Nathan Williams prove
uniform parking-object counts and bijections, not this prescribed
$W\times C$ character~\cite[Theorems~1.8 and~8.17]{GLTW}. Yosuke Ito
and Soichi Okada classify when generalized parking class functions are
$W$-characters or permutation characters \cite[Theorem~1.4]{IO}; see
also the construction question in \cite[Section~7.2]{IO}. Jason Stack
extends the parking-space construction to well-generated complex
reflection groups, with $G_{34}$, $E_7$ and $E_8$ expressly excluded
from the hypotheses of the main results
\cite[Theorems~1.3 and 3.19]{Stack}. Jason Stack and Nathan Williams
determine which degrees and carried representations occur for faithful
$W$-stable homogeneous systems of parameters of common positive degree
for nontrivial irreducible complex reflection groups
\cite[Theorem~1.6]{SW}. Extending work of Ito and Okada, they show that
the corresponding homogeneous quotient is, after forgetting the grading,
a permutation $W$-module if and only if the parameter space carries
$V^*$ \cite[Theorem~1.8]{SW}. Their Section~1.2 states that
``T.~Douvropoulos and M.~Josuat-Verg\`es completed the character
formula for noncrossing $(mh+1)$-parking spaces in types $E_7$ and
$E_8$''~\cite[Section~1.2]{SW}, citing~\cite[Theorems~3.10
and~3.12]{DJV}; on the scope of that computation, see the
quotation from~\cite[Section~3.2]{DJV} in the introduction. Jason
Stack's public research code, posted October 23, 2026, ``explores the
action of the underlying group on its noncrossing parking functions via
left multiplication, and its cyclic action for $k=1$''
\cite[README]{StackCode}.

\section{Action conventions}\label{app:conventions}
The row-matrix calculation is identified with the left geometric
action in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} as follows. GAP
permutations act on the right, and its conjugation notation is
$g^a=a^{-1}ga$. If $M(g)$ is the computed row matrix, the associated
left action is $v\mapsto vM(g)^{-1}$. Its fixed spaces equal those
of $M(g)$. The left Coxeter action traverses the flat orbits in the
opposite direction from the row-matrix traversal; their members and
lengths are unchanged. The abstract left-coset condition remains
$x^{-1}wx\in P_Xc^d$. Finally, the matrices are real, so the
multiplicities of any eigenvalue and its complex conjugate agree.
Inverting $M(w)$ therefore leaves the multiplicity of $\omega^d$
unchanged. These facts account for the direction and inverse
conventions in both sides of~\eqref{eq:main}.
