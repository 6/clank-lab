# Further credits and source notes

## References

- Cyriac Antony, Laavanya D. and Devi Yamini S., *Graceful coloring is computationally hard*, [arXiv:2407.02179v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02179v2) (July 2024). Theorems 4–6 settle graceful 5-colorability on 3-regular 3-connected planar bipartite graphs, graceful $k$-colorability for $k \ge 6$ on planar bipartite graphs of maximum degree $k-2$, and graceful 4-colorability on 2-degenerate graphs of maximum degree 3. The abstract ends: "The complexity of checking whether a planar graph is graceful 4-colorable remains open."
- Cyriac Antony, Jacob Antony, Laavanya D. and Devi Yamini S., *Graceful coloring is computationally hard*, CALDAM 2025, Lecture Notes in Computer Science, [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-83438-7_2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-83438-7_2). Its published abstract ends with the same sentence.
- Édouard Bonnet, Dibyayan Chakraborty and Julien Duron, *Cutting Barnette graphs perfectly is hard*, Theoretical Computer Science 1010 (2024), 114701, [doi:10.1016/j.tcs.2024.114701](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2024.114701), [arXiv:2302.11667v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11667v1). Theorem 1: "Perfect Matching Cut is NP-hard in 3-connected cubic bipartite planar graphs." The paper uses the construction of Section 3.2, the cubicity, planarity and bipartiteness parts of the proof of Lemma 13, and Lemmas 26 and 28 with the lemmas they rest on (numbering of arXiv version 1). The assumption of Section 3.2 that no variable is a cut vertex of the incidence graph is used only in the proof of Lemma 12 (3-connectivity), which the paper does not need.
- Andreas Darmann and Janosch Döcker, *On a simple hard variant of Not-All-Equal 3-Sat*, Theoretical Computer Science 815 (2020), 147–152, [doi:10.1016/j.tcs.2020.02.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2020.02.010): the source problem of the Bonnet–Chakraborty–Duron reduction.
- Z. Bi, A. Byers, S. English, E. Laforge and P. Zhang, *Graceful colorings of graphs*, J. Combin. Math. Combin. Comput. 101 (2017), 101–119: the definition of graceful coloring.
- Paola T. Pantoja, Simone Dantas and Atílio G. Luiz: *Bounds on graceful k-colorings of graphs*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 371 (2025), 218–231, [doi:10.1016/j.dam.2025.04.032](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2025.04.032); *Graceful colorings of graphs with maximum degree three*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 374 (2025), 75–96, [doi:10.1016/j.dam.2025.05.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2025.05.013); *On graceful colorings in subcubic trees*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 381 (2026), 98–111, [doi:10.1016/j.dam.2025.11.027](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2025.11.027).
- Paola Tatiana Pantoja Huaynoca, *Graceful colorings of graphs: structure, bounds, and complexity*, doctoral thesis, Universidade Federal Fluminense (March 2026), [open copy](https://sipos.app/pgmat/administracao/defesas/documentos/2026.03.23-14.56.04Tese%20Paola%20Tatiana%20Pantoja%20H.pdf). Lemma 6, attributed there to the subcubic-trees paper, says that in a graceful $(\Delta+1)$-coloring every vertex of degree $\Delta$ gets color $1$ or $\Delta+1$; its case $\Delta = 3$ is the first claim of the paper's Lemma 3. Theorem 10 (graceful 5-coloring NP-complete on 3-connected bipartite cubic planar graphs) and Theorem 34 (graceful 4-coloring NP-complete on graphs of maximum degree 3) are attributed there to the second and third papers above.
- Laavanya D., Devi Yamini S. and Muhammad Faisal Nadeem, *Graceful colorings of a subclass of bipartite graphs*, in: Nonlinear Complex Systems, Computational Intelligence and Their Applications, Springer Proceedings in Mathematics & Statistics (2026), 237–247, [doi:10.1007/978-981-95-5891-9_18](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-95-5891-9_18): the latest graceful-coloring work found by two of the authors who posed the question; it computes the graceful chromatic number of bipartite graphs built by an operation called unfolding, and does not concern complexity.
- Vitaly A. Baransky, Ilia A. Nasyrov and Tatiana A. Senchonok, *Locks and forbidden configurations in 4-graceful trees*, Ural Mathematical Journal 12(1) (2026), 26–44, [doi:10.15826/umj.2026.1.003](https://doi.org/10.15826/umj.2026.1.003): recent work on 4-graceful trees, read for scope (statement of results, introduction and references); it concerns trees only.
- Lean 4 and [Mathlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4) provide the formal proof infrastructure.

## Sources not read in full

None of these could plausibly contain the planar four-color theorem, for the reasons given.

- The three Discrete Applied Mathematics papers of Pantoja, Dantas and Luiz are behind a subscription. Pantoja's thesis, defended after all three appeared, lists them as its main contributions and attributes each theorem to one of them. It records that the maximum-degree-three paper left open whether an arbitrary subcubic graph has a graceful 4-coloring (Section 5.4), so that paper has no four-color hardness result. The hardness theorem attributed to the subcubic-trees paper is stated for graphs of maximum degree three (Theorem 34), and its proof follows the incidence structure of an arbitrary formula without any planarity condition; the zbMATH review of that paper (Zbl 1582.05107) also describes it as NP-complete "even for graphs with maximum degree 3". Among complexity results, the thesis's closing summary (Chapter 6) lists those two hardness results and a polynomial-time algorithm for graceful 4-colorability of trees of maximum degree three (Theorem 33, attributed to the subcubic-trees paper). The bounds paper contributes upper bounds, snark values and a computation for small cubic graphs (Theorems 21, 23–25 and 35 of the thesis).
- The body of the CALDAM 2025 chapter: its published abstract states that the planar four-color case remains open.
- The extended version promised in the arXiv paper's introduction: not located; arXiv version 2 is the latest public text found.
- Alexandru Popa and Andrei Popa, *A polynomial time algorithm for computing the graceful coloring number on trees*, SSRN 7181532, [doi:10.2139/ssrn.7181532](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7181532): the abstract confines it to an algorithm on trees.
- Baransky, Nasyrov and Senchonok's earlier papers *4-graceful trees* (Trudy Inst. Mat. Mekh. UrO RAN 30(4), 2024) and *Nodal components of 4-graceful trees* (Siberian Electron. Math. Rep. 23(1), 2026), in Russian: by their titles and the later paper's account of them, they concern trees only, and graceful 4-colorability of trees of maximum degree three is decided in polynomial time (Theorem 33 of Pantoja's thesis), so a result on trees cannot give planar hardness.
- Laavanya D. and Devi Yamini S., *Graceful coloring of some corona graphs — an algorithmic approach*, Commun. Comb. Optim. 11(2) (2026), 315–334: not read; its title and the opening of its zbMATH summary concern graceful colorings of corona graphs, not complexity on planar graphs.

## Conventions

The arXiv paper gives two descriptions of a graceful coloring: one asks the differences to be injective on all edges inside each closed neighborhood, the other asks only for a proper edge coloring. They agree on triangle-free graphs, and every double subdivision is triangle-free (proved in Lean as `s2_triangle_free`), so the theorem holds under both descriptions. The question and our theorem allow disconnected graphs; the restriction to connected inputs is not treated.

## Examples checked in the build

The complete graph $K_4$ has no perfect matching cut, so its double subdivision (16 vertices) has no graceful 4-coloring. The triangular prism has one, its three rungs, so its double subdivision has a graceful 4-coloring. Both are checked in `lean/PublicChecks.lean` from the main theorem.
