# Context and reading limits

The prior question is Problem 1.2 in Kang, Jin and Wang,
[arXiv:1506.04629v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.04629v1), attributed
there to Lu, Wang, Wang, Bu, Montassier and Raspaud (2009), reference [7].
That reproduction, rather than the unavailable original Lu full text, is
the question statement used here. The paper gives exact version-specific
references for the question and the edge bound. No priority claim is made.

The retained bibliography credits Kang, Jin, Liu and Wang (journal order;
Jin, Kang, Liu and Wang in arXiv:2001.00166v1), Kang, Jin and Zhu, Dai,
Wang and Xu, Chen, Dai, Nie, Wang and Yu, and Xiao and Huang.
Full-paper coverage is incomplete. Chen, Dai, Nie, Wang and Yu's Theorem 1.4
was read in their paper; Dai, Wang and Xu's paper was read only through its
title in Chen and coauthors' reference list, and Xiao and Huang's only through
its title and metadata, so neither one's abstract or theorems were read. Ang and Leo's full text,
Bu, Wang and Wang's updated *On Coloring Problems*, and Li, Niu and Tian's
*On the strong Bordeaux Conjecture* remain unread. These are explicit
source-coverage gaps, including for any priority assessment.

## Neighboring statements and coloring notions

The paper's scope is ordinary proper three-colorability. The following
source locators preserve the mathematical distinctions from the earlier
edition:

- Kang, Jin and Wang, [arXiv:1506.04629v1, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.04629v1):
  “Every planar graph without cycles of length 4, 6, 9 is 3-colorable.”
  Its introduction, page 2, attributes the corresponding exclusion of
  lengths 4, 7 and 9 to Lu and coauthors, reference [7].
- Kang, Jin and Zhu, [arXiv:2303.04657v1, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04657v1):
  “Every planar graph without cycles of length 4, 7 or 9 is DP-3-colorable.”
  Corollary 1.2 gives three-choosability and hence ordinary three-colorability
  for that class. In general, DP-3-colorability implies three-choosability,
  which implies ordinary proper three-colorability. Failure of a list or
  DP coloring property alone would not supply the obstruction required here.
- A $(d_1,d_2,d_3)$-coloring permits each color class to induce maximum
  degree at most $d_j$. Dai, Wang and Xu's title is “Every planar graph
  without cycles of length 4 or 9 is (1,1,0)-colorable”
  ([publisher](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2017.04.006)). Chen, Dai, Nie,
  Wang and Yu's title is “Planar graphs without cycles of length 4 or 9
  are (2,0,0)-colorable” ([publisher](https://doi.org/10.1360/N012018-00129)).
  These defective colorings allow monochromatic edges and are compatible
  with the counterexample's failure of proper three-colorability.
- Xiao and Huang's paper, *A weak DP-coloring of planar graphs without 4- and
  9-cycles* ([publisher](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2023.10.004)), was read
  through its title and bibliographic metadata only; nothing about its
  content is asserted here.

The historical status statement is Problem 1.2 and its following paragraph
in Jin, Kang, Liu and Wang, [arXiv:2001.00166v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00166v1)
(2020); this is not a claim about current status of the other cases.
The abstract of Ang–Leo's 2026 conference paper describes two
nonplanar constructions, the second excluding lengths four, five and six;
it supplies no planar counterexample to the assertion considered here.
This reading is of the publisher abstract, not the full text. No conclusion
about priority is drawn from the unread sources.

## Discovery and illustration

The witness was found by composing small planar graphs with designated
contact vertices, tabulating the possible color patterns on those
contacts, and seeking a composition with no allowed pattern. This
explains the discovery route; the certificate checks apply to the final
edge list directly. The search is not needed for the proof and makes
no claim of exhaustive search, optimal order or applicability to another
forbidden pair.
