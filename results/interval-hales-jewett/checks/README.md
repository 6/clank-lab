# Further credits and source notes

## References

- David Conlon and Nina Kamčev, *Intervals in the Hales–Jewett theorem*, [arXiv:1801.08919v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08919v2) (July 2018); European Journal of Combinatorics 132 (2026), 104258, [doi:10.1016/j.ejc.2025.104258](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2025.104258). Quotations and the numbering of Question 3.1 follow arXiv version 2. The journal version was revised after it (its reference list adds Kamčev and Spiegel's 2022 paper), and its wording may differ. Theorem 1.1 gives, for any $n$ and any odd $r > 1$, an $r$-coloring of $[3]^n$ "containing no monochromatic combinatorial line whose wildcard set is the union of fewer than $r$ intervals". Section 3 records the upper bound from Shelah's proof and asks, as Question 3.1, whether there exist $m \ge 4$, $r \ge 2$ and $c > 1$ such that there are $r$-colorings of $[m]^n$ "containing no monochromatic combinatorial line whose wildcard set is the union of at most $cr$ intervals". Its Question 3.2, on the upper bound, is not addressed here.
- Imre Leader and Eero Räty, *A note on intervals in the Hales–Jewett theorem*, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 25(3) (2018), #P3.15, [doi:10.37236/7664](https://doi.org/10.37236/7664). Its Theorem 2 gives a monochromatic line with an interval as wildcard set for every 2-coloring of $[3]^n$, $n$ large; it disproves the conjecture of Conlon and Kamčev that, for any $n$, some 2-coloring of $[3]^n$ has "no monochromatic line whose active coordinate set is an interval".
- Nina Kamčev and Christoph Spiegel, *Another note on intervals in the Hales–Jewett theorem*, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 29(1) (2022), #P1.62, [doi:10.37236/9400](https://doi.org/10.37236/9400). Theorem 2 settles every even number of colors over three letters. Quotations and numbering follow the journal version; arXiv:1811.04628v1 numbers these results Theorem 1.2 and Question 3.1 and reads "r is the best explicit lower bound" where the journal version has $r - 1$. Section 3 defines $I(m, r)$, asks Question 19, whether one can improve on the bounds $\max\lbrace I(m-1, r), I(m, r-1)\rbrace \le I(m, r) \le \mathrm{HJ}(m-1, r)$, and adds: "In particular, $r - 1$ is the best explicit lower bound that the present methods can give on $I(m, r)$, which is probably far from the truth." The theorem here improves the lower bound; the upper-bound half of Question 19 stays open. 
- Younes Mouhib, *Lower bounds for the Hales–Jewett numbers via symmetric and one-weight colorings*, [arXiv:2606.22155v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22155v2) (July 24, 2026), Question 66, and *One-weight colorings, the symmetric class, and lower bounds for Hales–Jewett numbers*, [arXiv:2607.02226v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02226v1) (July 2, 2026), Question 8.9: "For $t \geq 4$ nothing is known beyond $\lambda(t, r) \leq \min\lbrace \lceil \mathrm{HJ}(t, r)/2 \rceil, \mathrm{HJ}(t-1, r)\rbrace - 1$", where $\lambda(t, r) = I(t, r) - 1$; their Propositions 51 and 7.2 show that colorings invariant under permuting positions cannot give a lower bound.
- Younes Mouhib, *Improving bounds on Hales–Jewett numbers: symmetric colorings, SAT solvers, line-family variants, and forcing structures*, master's thesis, ETH Zürich, dated July 6, 2026, [author's copy](https://ysmouhib.github.io/Mouhib_Master_Thesis.pdf), posted on the author's site on September 1, 2026. Question 4.21 repeats the passage above, with Proposition 4.15 in place of Proposition 51.
- A. W. Hales and R. I. Jewett, *Regularity and positional games*, Transactions of the American Mathematical Society 106 (1963), 222–229, [doi:10.1090/S0002-9947-1963-0143712-1](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1963-0143712-1): the theorem itself.
- Saharon Shelah, *Primitive recursive bounds for van der Waerden numbers*, Journal of the American Mathematical Society 1 (1988), 683–697, [doi:10.1090/S0894-0347-1988-0929498-X](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-1988-0929498-X): the proof that gives the upper bound $I(m, r) \le \mathrm{HJ}(m-1, r)$.
- Lean 4 and [Mathlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4) provide the formal proof infrastructure.
