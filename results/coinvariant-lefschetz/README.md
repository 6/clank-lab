# The strong Lefschetz property for complex reflection coinvariants

[![Checks](https://github.com/6/clank-lab/actions/workflows/coinvariant-lefschetz.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/6/clank-lab/actions/workflows/coinvariant-lefschetz.yml)

A [complex reflection group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_reflection_group) acts on polynomials, and its coinvariant ring is the quotient obtained by setting all positive-degree invariant polynomials to zero. The strong Lefschetz property asks for one linear form whose powers give maps of the largest possible rank between every pair of homogeneous pieces. Wachi's question for these rings is documented in [2013](https://doi.org/10.34508/repsympo.2013.0_140). Earlier work established all irreducible types except the exceptional group $G_{34}$. This work proves that case by exact certificates, completing the irreducible classification. The paper then removes fixed coordinates and uses the tensor-product theorem to prove the assertion for every finite complex reflection group over $\mathbb{C}$.

The result builds on Harima and Watanabe's monomial-family theorem, the rank-two theorem of Harima, Migliore, Nagel and Watanabe, McDaniel's real-reflection theorem, and Wachi's computations for eight exceptional types, including $G_{33}$. The new proof follows McDaniel's relative-coinvariant strategy for real reflection groups and uses Conway and Sloane's construction as developed by Oura and Sekiguchi, together with the free-extension results of Harima and Watanabe and of McDaniel, Chen, Iarrobino and Macias Marques.

## Abstract

Wachi conjectured in 2013 that the coinvariant algebra of every finite complex reflection group has the strong Lefschetz property: some linear form has powers of maximal rank between all graded pieces. Work of Harima–Watanabe, Harima–Migliore–Nagel–Watanabe, McDaniel, and Wachi settles every irreducible type except the exceptional group $G_{34}$. We prove the $G_{34}$ case, and hence the conjecture over $\mathbb{C}$. Smith announced the $G_{34}$ case in 2016, as part of a Bott–Samelson argument for groups generated by reflections of order two; we know of no written proof of it, and our proof does not use it. We follow McDaniel's strategy for real reflection groups: the coinvariant algebra of $G_{34}$ is a free extension of the $756$-dimensional relative coinvariant algebra of the subgroup $G_{33}$, with the $G_{33}$ coinvariant algebra as fiber. Using the Conway–Sloane invariants in the coordinates of Oura–Sekiguchi, we prove the strong Lefschetz property of the relative algebra from exact polynomial identities over $`\mathbb{F}_{1009}`$, lifted to characteristic zero by specialization and a Hilbert-function comparison. The free-extension theorem of Harima–Watanabe and McDaniel–Chen–Iarrobino–Macias Marques then transfers it to $G_{34}$, using Wachi's result for $G_{33}$.

This work has not yet been reviewed by experts. The proofs, the certificate checkers and the text were produced by AI models (from OpenAI and Anthropic), working under the author's direction.

## Check it

Use Python 3.11 or later, a C++17 compiler with OpenMP, FLINT and GMP development libraries, Singular 4, and GNU time. On Debian or Ubuntu, install the native requirements with `sudo apt-get install g++ libflint-dev libgmp-dev singular time python3-venv`. From this folder:

```sh
python3 -m venv .venv
. .venv/bin/activate
python3 -m pip install -r checks/requirements.txt
python3 checks/verify.py --workers 2
```

The command reconstructs the large transformation witness locally from the included recipe, then checks it against the supplied input and basis certificates with both independent arithmetic implementations. It also checks the group action, input identities, finite monomial cover, multiplication ranks, and rejection of altered certificates. The paper proves the reduction to characteristic zero and applies the cited results for the other types; these arguments are not checked by Lean. Allow several gigabytes of temporary disk space. The paper source builds with XeLaTeX and the IPA Mincho font. See [the checker guide](checks/README.md) for the format, individual checks, and trusted software.

## Credits

Akihito Wachi posed the question in 2013 and proved the eight exceptional cases used here, including $G_{33}$. Wachi's [repository](https://github.com/wachiakihito/slp-cpx-refl-gp/tree/f2563d1c40ed2efb138bc6e4faaae28a0a19d608) states: "We have no proof for the remaining one case G34 (rank 6), but it is natural to conjecture it has the strong Lefschetz property." Wachi's [grant report for fiscal year 2024](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/ja/report/KAKENHI-PROJECT-22K03347/22K033472024jisseki/) says that for this group the property can be decided if a certain relation among its basic invariants holds, and that the relation itself is unproved (our translation). Larry Smith announced at [BIRS in 2016](https://archive.birs.ca/files/2016/16w5114/report16w5114.pdf) that a Bott–Samelson embedding gives the strong Lefschetz property when all reflections have order two, a class containing $G_{34}$; Chris McDaniel and Smith later showed that the property "need not be inherited" along such an embedding. Our proof takes a different route.

The proof follows McDaniel's relative-coinvariant free-extension strategy and uses the free-extension theorems of Tadahito Harima and Junzo Watanabe and of McDaniel, Shujian Chen, Anthony Iarrobino and Pedro Macias Marques, and John Conway and Neil Sloane's construction as developed by Manabu Oura and Jiro Sekiguchi. The other types rest on work of Harima, Watanabe, Juan Migliore, Uwe Nagel, McDaniel and Wachi. The checkers use SymPy, FLINT, GMP and Singular. The [checker guide](checks/README.md#further-credits) gives further credits.
