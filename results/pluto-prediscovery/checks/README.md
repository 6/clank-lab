# Checks

## What the scripts do

- `rerun.py WORK` is the entry point. It downloads every input from public services into `WORK`, repeats the measurements and controls listed below as "rerun", writes `WORK/results.json` and compares 62 of its numbers with [../data/expected.json](../data/expected.json), which gives each recorded value and its tolerance. It exits nonzero if any falls outside; other numbers in `results.json` are not checked. The random-position tests use fixed seeds, so they reproduce exactly. It also saves the inputs of the figure and the blinded sheet, which `figure.py` draws.
- `plates.py` is Method B: plate solution, matched filter, centroid, photometry, the replay of the measurement at shifted positions (`replay`), comparably faint stars, the same stars held out of the fits (`heldout`), and injections, both the version-1 kind added after filtering (`inject`) and through the whole measurement (`inject_pipeline`).
- `clock.py` dates an exposure from the trail of a known asteroid.
- `census.py WORK` lists the public plates covering Pluto's track from 1880 to June 1909 and writes `WORK/census.json` and `WORK/census-exposures.csv` (released as [../data/census-exposures.csv](../data/census-exposures.csv)).
- `ps1_detection.py WORK [OUT.pdf]` rereads the single Pan-STARRS z detection 0.9″ from the December image: its MAST records and flags, and forced fluxes at its position in the z warps of its own and the preceding night and in the z stack (about 135 MB of PS1 images). It writes `WORK/ps1_detection.json` and, with `OUT.pdf`, the paper's Figure 2 (released as [../data/ps1-detection.pdf](../data/ps1-detection.pdf)).
- `figure.py WORK OUT.svg` draws the figure from the stamps `rerun.py` saves; `figure.py WORK OUT.pdf --blind KEY.json` draws the blinded sheet and writes its key.

Versions are pinned in `requirements.txt`; `figure.py` and `ps1_detection.py`'s figure also need matplotlib. The service calls retry when Harvard's API asks clients to slow down. VizieR queries retry with backoff and try the CDS mirror last. For APASS the check also carries pinned cuts, [../data/apass/](../data/apass/), with their SHA-256 hashes in [pinned.json](../data/apass/pinned.json): when VizieR answers, the check confirms that the answer matches the pinned cut and stops if it does not; when VizieR fails after its retries, it uses the pinned cut after verifying its hash. Either way it prints, and saves under `apass_source` in `results.json`, which source each plate used. Setting `PLUTO_FORCE_VIZIER_FAILURE=1` makes the APASS query fail, to test the fallback.

## Clock corrections

Harvard's logbook start and stop readings for MA 731 were not available to us. The storage envelope gives "Dec. 20, 1908" (the local evening), "60m" and quality 4. DASCH's data guide says that Harvard exposure times "were generally recorded in Local Sidereal Time", and that the reported time is "the average of the start and stop times". The archive midpoint is 06:46:36.5 UT with a 60-minute exposure.

Trail fits, 1σ errors (the fit's covariance, inflated by 1 + 2 Σρ for the correlation of neighboring minutes). The errors hold only if the trail model, a band with blurred start and stop edges, is right; they are not bounds on the true times.

Of the eight fainter asteroids, 4 of the 7 testable on MA 731 (Salonta, McGetchin, Barsig, Samantha) show excess signal in the corrected window (p ≤ 0.0041, at most 1 of 246 comparison windows), and none of the 5 testable in the archive window; on MA 733, 4 of 8 do (McGetchin, Andrea Doria, Samantha, Urabe). An earlier version reported "9 of 15" pooled over both plates; the recorded tests give 8 of 15. The (179) fit reads only a square of ±700 pixels (±0.2°) around the asteroid's track. That square is 5.4° from Pluto, and on the Heidelberg plates the (417) square is 2.3° from Pluto. `rerun.py` records both distances, so no pixel near Pluto enters a clock. The clock was fitted, and checked against the other asteroids, before Pluto's position at the corrected time was measured.

In arcseconds along Pluto's track: on MA 731 the prediction moves 0.049″ per minute. The fit's 0.7-minute error moves it by 0.03″, and the 6-minute spread between asteroids by 0.3″. The fitted exposure lasts 73 minutes against the envelope's 60; a 60-minute exposure that began at the fitted opening or ended at the fitted closing would move the midpoint by 6.5 minutes, or 0.32″. At Heidelberg it moves 0.017″ per minute, so 20 minutes is 0.35″.

A logged sidereal start time (a clock set by the stars) read as local mean time (a clock set by the Sun) reproduces the archive midpoint to 2 minutes, a hypothesis not tested against the logbook. On the Heidelberg plates Method B's fits to the trails of (168) Sibylla and (179) Klytaemnestra gave no clean fit: their fitted durations, 3.7 to 7.2 hours, exceed the 3-hour exposure. `rerun.py` reports the (168) fits but does not use them. Method B adopts the archive midpoints there (20:20:11 and 20:20:23 UT). Method A fitted (168) Sibylla and (417) Suevia on both plates and (179) Klytaemnestra on B2248b, and adopts per-plate medians of 20:17:45 and 20:07:21 UT; the two choices move Pluto's prediction by at most 0.22″.

## Method B (rerun)

For each plate: a cut-out of about 70′ around the prediction; a cubic plate solution against Gaia DR3 propagated to the plate epoch; a Gaussian matched filter whose width is fitted on APASS stars of B 14–16; S/N against the robust scatter of the filtered scan at random points; a free centroid: the brightest filtered pixel within 8 pixels of the rounded predicted pixel, then a Gaussian-weighted centroid (weights from the unfiltered scan, clipped at zero) in the 15 × 15 pixel box around it, three iterations, so the final center can lie up to about 7 pixels beyond the 8-pixel circle and its distance is not limited; B from an APASS calibration quadratic in the logarithm of the peak, with a color term, evaluated at B − V = 0.78 and restricted to the calibration's monotonic branch. The same code and parameters are used on every plate. The restriction to the monotonic branch was added when the calibration on B2247a turned over near its faint end; it affects only the expected S/N and the brightness of injected sources, not any measured position, S/N or magnitude.

## Method A (recorded, not rerun)

Method A was written separately. On MA 731 it fits a local plane, cataloged neighbors and a profile trailed along Pluto's motion, with the profile and response fitted on stars away from the target and the target pixels masked until its rules were frozen. Its rules: amplitude above the 99th percentile of 200 matched translated tracks, centroid within 6″, B within 0.45 mag of the prediction. On the Heidelberg plates a second implementation, written without sight of the other measurements, uses the archive's plate solution with a local affine correction, empirical profiles and Gaia synthetic B magnitudes, under the same rules. Its values are in Table 1 of the paper. Its MA 731 center is RA 85.893481°, Dec +16.016531°; its Heidelberg centers, reconstructed from its recorded offsets, are 84.961402°, +16.133058° (B2247a) and 84.961295°, +16.133450° (B2248b). Against Method B's predictions these are 0.22″, 1.68″ and 0.87″ off; against its own adopted times, 0.24″, 1.68″ and 1.06″. The two methods' centers differ by 0.9″ on MA 731 and by 1.4″ and 2.1″ on the Heidelberg plates. Method A finds the images 0.07 to 0.3 mag brighter. Its depth (faintest tested B at which at least 80% of 200 inserted images pass its amplitude trigger; its centroid and brightness conditions are not applied, so this overstates its depth, and the B3384b verdict "too shallow" from the same test is conservative) is at least 17.00 on MA 731 and at least 17.70 on B2247a, where the faintest grid point still passed, and 17.00 on B2248b. Its brightness rule is B within 0.45 mag of the prediction; on MA 731 its 15.96 is 0.37 mag brighter than 16.33.

## Controls

Rerun by `rerun.py`:

- The archive-time position on MA 731, 17.8″ from the image: forced S/N −0.2; the same free search as at the image ends 11.3″ from that position, drawn toward the candidate image (6.6″ from it, S/N 4.3), outside the 6″ limit.
- Injected trailed sources at Pluto's B ± 0.5 mag on each plate, two ways. Version 1 added them to the filtered scan and scored the largest S/N within 3 pixels (99% at S/N ≥ 3 on MA 731); that skips the centroid, position and brightness steps. Version 2 also adds them to the unfiltered scan, filters again and runs the whole measurement at the injected position: recovered means S/N ≥ 4 at the fitted center, the center within 6″ and B within 0.45 mag. At Pluto's B, 200 positions per plate: 72.5% on MA 731, 79% on B2247a, 76% on B2248b, 84% on B3383a and 81.5% on B4816a; at 0.5 mag fainter, 9.5–15.5% on the 1908–09 plates. Nearly every injection at Pluto's B reaches S/N 4 (97–98.5%); the losses are in position and brightness, mostly near brighter stars. The same positions without an injection pass S/N ≥ 4 within 6″ in 0.5–3.5% of cases. Each position is also read as a forced measurement, at the injected position displaced by a random 1″ rms registration error per axis: at Pluto's B, 98.5–100% reach S/N ≥ 3 (median 5.2–7.5); without an injection, 6.5–16% reach S/N 2. The bright-end losses are attributed to nearby brighter stars only as a hypothesis. The paper's Appendix B has the table.
- The nearest Gaia DR3 star to each image, with Gaia moved to the plate epoch as everywhere else (version 1 used the unmoved 2016 positions here; the same star, 0.007–0.028″ farther): 11.38″ (G 18.86) on MA 731, 25.63″ (G 16.14) on B2247a.
- IMCCE SkyBoT, one query per epoch with radius 0.04°: geocentric at the trail-based midpoint for the December image, and from Heidelberg (code 024) at 20:20 UT for the February position. Pluto alone each time. The wider and three-time searches below are recorded; each recorded query's observer, time, radius and answer is in [../data/skybot-recorded.json](../data/skybot-recorded.json).
- The joint test (`plates.replay`): 300,000 random sky offsets (seed 1909), each applied to all three predictions; offsets are uniform in a ±1500″ square in each tangent-plane coordinate, those within 60″ of zero are dropped, and 230,352 put the shifted prediction at least 60 pixels inside all three cut-outs. At each moved prediction the measurement itself is replayed: the brightest filtered pixel within 8 pixels, its windowed centroid, the S/N there, the centroid's distance from the moved prediction and a Gaia check within 3″ of the centroid. The thresholds are the replay's values at the real predictions (S/N 6.25, 4.84, 5.84; distances 0.65″, 0.31″, 1.90″). Results:
  - As close and as strong as observed on all three plates: 0 of 230,352 (95% upper bound 1.3 × 10⁻⁵); also 0 counting only centroids with no Gaia star within 3″.
  - On single plates: 29 on MA 731 (1.3 × 10⁻⁴), 6 on B2247a (2.6 × 10⁻⁵), 223 on B2248b (9.7 × 10⁻⁴); 1 on both February plates, 0 on both counting only centroids with no Gaia star within 3″; 2 on MA 731 alone with no Gaia star within 3″.
  - The search's own, looser acceptance rule, S/N ≥ 4 at the centroid wherever it falls: 8,581 offsets match all three plates, and 3,360 (1.5%) of them pass the Gaia veto on every plate separately: on each plate, no Gaia star (G < 19.5) within 3″ of that plate's centroid. An earlier version of this test, which counted entries of a 9-pixel local-maximum list within a sky radius and used their S/N at the integer peak, reported 129 and 0; the exact replay corrects it. Their centroids lie a median 11.8″, 11.4″ and 11.7″ from the moved prediction (rerun). A plausible explanation, not tested by the replay, is that the brightest filtered pixel lies on a bright star's outer profile and the centroid moves toward the star yet stays more than 3″ from it. On single plates 12–15% of offsets pass this rule with no Gaia star within 3″; over half of those have a Gaia star of G < 16 within 20″.
  - Requiring, after seeing these counts, the centroid also to lie within 3″ of the moved prediction: 0 of 230,352. This cut was chosen after seeing the data.
  - Crowding matched: offsets where every plate's count of Gaia stars (G < 19.5) within 60″ is within max(2, round(0.2 n)) of the count n at Pluto (n = 25, 18 and 18; admitted 20–30 and 14–22, so 22% on the Heidelberg plates). With 20% taken literally (15–21): 0 of 31,434 (bound 9.5 × 10⁻⁵). As close and as strong as observed on all three: 0 of 39,786 (bound 7.5 × 10⁻⁵); the loose rule with no Gaia star within 3″: 722.
  - The outside review of version 1 noted that the 8-pixel limit binds only the brightest filtered pixel. Requiring, after seeing the counts, the final centroid also to lie within 8 pixels of the prediction, for the real measurement (0.6, 0.3 and 1.9 pixels) and every shifted one alike: 201 loose-rule matches on all three plates, none of which passes the Gaia veto on every plate. The published rule is unchanged.
  - The zero is a count of trials meeting six conditions together (on each plate, S/N at least and distance at most the observed values); it and its 95% upper bound describe the per-trial frequency of that event. The 3,360 of 230,352 (1.46%) under the loose rule shows how strongly such a count depends on the match definition. Neither is a significance level or a probability that the candidates are Pluto. They are per trial and conditional on the three plates and times reported, their observed thresholds and these definitions. They leave out the other plates and times examined (MA 733, and the archive times of MA 731 and MA 733) and how these three were selected, and they do not model plate defects. The joint count does not confirm December: the February pair alone matches in 1 trial and MA 731 alone in 29.
- Comparably faint stars, measured exactly as the images: isolated APASS stars within 0.5 mag of Pluto's B and 1,500 pixels of the prediction, with the Gaia position as truth. In version 1 they also entered the plate solution and the calibration (median miss 1.24″, 1.03″ and 1.23″; images at the 20th, 6th and 73rd percentiles; B spreads 0.21, 0.24 and 0.17 mag). Version 2 holds them out (`heldout`): in five random groups, each group is measured with a cubic solution solved again from the archive's starting solution with its Gaia stars removed before any matching, clipping or fitting, and a calibration without its APASS stars. Held out, the median miss is 1.24″ on MA 731 (193 stars), 1.03″ on B2247a (146) and 1.22″ on B2248b (124); the images fall at the 20th, 5th and 73rd percentiles. Median residuals east, north: −0.29″, −0.32″; +0.10″, −0.03″; +0.02″, +0.03″, with robust spreads of 0.9–1.1″ per axis. Calibrated minus catalog B: median +0.04, +0.09 and +0.04 mag, robust spread 0.21, 0.25 and 0.18 mag. All stars enter the B statistics. They use each star's own APASS color; with the images' B − V = 0.78 instead, median +0.02, +0.10 and +0.06 mag, spread 0.21, 0.24 and 0.18 mag. Still shared with the full fit: the archive's starting solution, the selection of the stars (made with the full solution), the noise scale and the matched filter's width, fitted on APASS stars of B 14–16, which can include a few of them.

## Census (`census.py`)

Pluto's track from Horizons, daily, with B = V + 0.87 (the redder assumed color). Pluto's track is sampled every 30 days; a sample is skipped when Pluto is within 30° of the Sun on the sample date. At each kept sample DASCH is queried for exposures with midpoints within 15 days that cover Pluto's position at the sample date, whatever the elongation on their own dates; exposures only in the windows of skipped samples are not queried. Each returned exposure is placed against Pluto's position on its own date: "inside" when DASCH's edge distance at the sample point exceeds Pluto's distance from that point, "edge" (unresolved) when it is equal or smaller, "unknown" when DASCH gives no edge distance. A plate that covers Pluto on its date but not the sample point is missed. A row is one plate solution's exposure; an exposure returned by several samples is kept once with its best label, and merging the rows of different solutions of the same exposure leaves every count unchanged (`census.json`, `merged_solutions`). The depth margin is DASCH's limiting magnitude (APASS, else ATLAS) minus Pluto's B, both unrounded (B is rounded only in printed and exported copies); a missing limit is tested before the comparison, and a margin of zero or more passes. With B − V = 0.87 no margin lies within 0.17 mag of zero; with 0.78 the closest is MA 733 at −0.09 mag. [../data/census-exposures.csv](../data/census-exposures.csv) lists all 1,906 DASCH exposures counted (1,746 inside and 11 at the edge before December 1908, 149 inside after), one row per exposure: plate, date (UTC), series, plate solution, coverage, limit, Pluto's B, margin (to 0.001 mag) and verdict (passes, short, or not tested with the reason). It is regenerated by `census.py`; SHA-256 `1d2dda36fd391404ac68e959cc6cbec3b8637bc6c083087490be38fa8d9d3e8e`. With the combined 1933–34 color, B − V = 0.78, every margin rises by 0.09 mag; no further exposure passes (`census.json`, `margin_ge_0_with_BV_0.78`). Heidelberg plates centered at RA 30°–110°, Dec −10° to +35°, dated before 1909-06-30 00:00 UT, are placed through their archived plate solutions; the archive gives no limiting magnitude. APPLAUSE DR4 exposures with a recorded time and pointing are listed with their distance from Pluto.

- December 1908 to June 1909: 149 DASCH exposures inside, 0 edge, 0 unknown; 56 have a limit. Only MA 731 passes (+0.47); MA 733 is 0.18 mag short. Heidelberg has only B2247a and B2248b. At least 80% of 200 images about 0.5 mag fainter than Pluto inserted there pass Method A's amplitude threshold (not its full rule); through Method B's whole measurement 79% and 76% are recovered at Pluto's B and about 10% at 0.5 mag fainter.
- Before December 1908: 1,746 DASCH exposures inside (1886 to 1908), 11 edge, 0 unknown; 525 of the 1,746 have a limit, and none passes. The best, I 31266 (December 14, 1903, 63 minutes), has a limit 0.76 mag brighter than Pluto's B and was not measured; a resampled cut-out estimator, whose S/N scale differs from the native-pixel values of the measurements, expects S/N 3.6 there and 4.1 on MA 731. Eight solved deep-series plates without a DASCH limit fail a depth screen on their scans. 70 plates of the deep B, C and I series of 30 minutes or more have no plate solution and were not screened. The remaining exposures without a limit are unassessed.
- Heidelberg before December 1908: ten plates from Wolf's double astrograph (G1120, G1123–G1125 of 1896; G2391–G2393 and G2420–G2422 of 1901), which did not reliably recover faint comparison stars near Pluto's position, and the Bruce plate B342a (1901), where Pluto lies 75 pixels from the scan edge and the cut-out could not be registered.
- APPLAUSE: 34 exposures with a mid-exposure year before 1909.5, all from Potsdam (1893–1902), all with a time and pointing and all used; the nearest nominal pointing is 22.9° from Pluto.

## Plates without public scans

- We found no public scan of the 1903 Franklin-Adams plate. For the Yerkes plates of 1909 the 1998 report gave estimated positions, but we located no published numerical astrometry and no full calibrated scan; a marked reproduction of the November 11, 1909 plate accompanies T. Reichhardt's 2015 Air & Space account of W. Osborn and F. Mills's inspection.
- The Harvard MB duplicate plates of December 20 and 21, 1908, noted on the storage envelopes, have no full DASCH scan and were not tested. The one scanned and solved MB plate covering the position, MB 2691, reaches APASS B 13.8, which suggests they would be too shallow.
