\begin{abstract}
We prove $R(P_{10},P_{10},P_{10})=18$: every three-coloring of the edges
of $K_{18}$ contains a monochromatic path on ten vertices, and some
three-coloring of $K_{17}$ contains none. Faudree and Schelp suggested a
formula for three-color path Ramsey numbers. Gyárfás, Ruszinkó, Sárközy
and Szemerédi proved that the three-color Ramsey number of the path on
$n$ vertices is $2n-2+(n\bmod2)$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
Dybizbański, Dzido and Radziszowski established the eight- and
nine-vertex cases, completing the cases through nine vertices recorded
by Radziszowski's survey. The lower-bound coloring is the even-case
$K_4$ blow-up given by Gyárfás, Ruszinkó, Sárközy and Szemerédi and by
Dybizbański, Dzido and Radziszowski. The new upper bound follows from
Boolean satisfiability refutations after fixing a monochromatic
nine-vertex path and imposing simultaneous symmetry constraints. A
separate refutation proves that the cases considered cover every
coloring. The reduction and the certificate checks are formalized in Lean.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
For graphs $G_1,G_2,G_3$, the Ramsey number $R(G_1,G_2,G_3)$ is the least
$N$ such that every coloring of the edges of $K_N$ with colors $1,2,3$
contains, for some $i$, a copy of $G_i$ all of whose edges have color $i$.
We write $\R(G)=R(G,G,G)$, and $P_n$ for the path on $n$ vertices (with
$n-1$ edges). Paths are not required to be induced.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
$\R(P_{10})=18$. That is, every coloring of the edges of $K_{18}$ with
three colors contains a monochromatic path on $10$ vertices, and some
$3$-coloring of the edges of $K_{17}$ contains none.
\end{theorem}

The advance is the upper bound $\R(P_{10})\le18$.
Gyárfás, Ruszinkó, Sárközy and Szemerédi proved
$\R(P_n)=2n-2+(n\bmod2)$ for all sufficiently large $n$
\cite[Theorem~1]{GRSS07}, with its corrigendum.
Dybizbański, Dzido and Radziszowski proved $\R(P_8)=14$
(Theorem~10) and $\R(P_9)\le17$ (Theorem~8), which with their
lower bound gives $\R(P_9)=17$ \cite{DDR16}.
Radziszowski's survey \cite{Rad26}
records the formula through $n=9$ and $P_{10}$ as the first open case.
Our $K_{17}$ lower bound is the even-case blow-up in
\cite{GRSS07}, also given in
\cite{DDR16}.
In Conjecture~2 of \cite{DDR16}, Dybizbański, Dzido and Radziszowski
attribute the suggestion to Faudree and Schelp \cite{FS75}.

\begin{conjecture}[suggested by Faudree--Schelp~\cite{FS75}; stated as a conjecture in~\cite{DDR16}; see~\cite{Rad26}]\label{conj:FS}
For all $n\ge1$, $\R(P_n)=2n-2+(n\bmod 2)$.
\end{conjecture}

This paper settles $n=10$ of Conjecture~\ref{conj:FS}; the general
conjecture remains open. Starting from their nine-vertex bound, our
upper-bound computation combines simultaneous symmetry constraints with
a separately verified cover of the remaining cases. The difficult step is obtaining a tractable case
split without losing a coloring: fixing a guaranteed path and imposing
symmetry constraints must be jointly sound, and the case division must
be proved complete. A lexicographic minimum and a separate refutation
excluding all assignments outside the selected cases discharge these
obligations. Dybizbański, Dzido and Radziszowski reported that their
attempts at the ten- and eleven-vertex cases remained incomplete \cite{DDR16}.

The method combines cube-and-conquer with symmetry breaking and verified
certificate checking. Heule, Kullmann, Wieringa and Biere's
cube-and-conquer method divides a satisfiability problem into subproblems
\cite{HKWB12}. For certificate import we use Böving et al.'s verified
LRAT checker \cite{BBC+25}. LRAT certificates are propositional
refutations whose steps refer to earlier clauses by number.
Szeider's streaming tool \texttt{lrat-catcher} imports certificates into
Lean theorems \cite{Sze26}; our import uses the same checker with its own
compact encoding. Kirchweger, Manrique and Szeider combine SAT modulo
symmetries and Lean for formally verified graph generation \cite{KMS26}.

Computational precedents include Heule, Kullmann and Marek's
Boolean Pythagorean triples proof \cite[Theorem~1]{HKM16}, Codish, Frank,
Itzhakov and Miller's $R(4,3,3)=30$ \cite{CFIM16}, and Heule and
Scheucher's empty-hexagon theorem \cite{HS24}, with Subercaseaux et al.'s
formal verification \cite{SNG+24}. In related Ramsey computations, Wesley
proves $R(C_3,C_6,C_6)=R(C_5,C_6,C_6)=15$
\cite[Theorems~3 and~4]{Wes25}; Galindez reports
$R(C_{10},C_{10},C_{10})\le26$ \cite{Gal26}, using
cube-and-conquer and Kirchweger--Szeider's SAT modulo symmetries
\cite{KS24}. Shao, Jiang, Xu and Wang provide a
reproduction package for $R(C_4,C_6,C_8)=12$ \cite{SJXW26}, and
Leo-Y-Zhang provides \textit{CycleRamsey} \cite{Zha26}.
Zhang's tree-bound corollary $R_k(T)\le k(n-2)+2$ for trees on $n\ge2$
vertices \cite{ZhJ26} comes from the GPT-6 Astra
proof packaged by Adamczewski; it gives $\R(P_{10})\le26$.

Earlier path and cycle literature includes Dzido \cite{Dzi05},
Dzido--Fidytek \cite{DF09}, Omidi--Raeisi \cite{OR11},
Maherani et al.\ \cite[Theorem~1.1]{MORS15}, Benevides--Skokan
\cite{BS09}, Figaj--Łuczak \cite{FL07} and Letzter \cite{Let22}.
For small cases, see Irving, Yang--Rowlinson and Yang
\cite{Irv74,YR94,YR92,Yan95}, and Radziszowski's survey
\cite{Rad26}.
The extremal background is due to Erdős--Gallai \cite{EG59},
Faudree--Schelp \cite{FS75}, whose theorem is recalled by Dybizbański,
Dzido and Radziszowski \cite[Theorem~4]{DDR16}. See also Kopylov's work on paths and cycles \cite{Kop77}.
Allen et al.\ study paths and cycles with few color changes
\cite{ABC+26}.
We use de~Moura and Ullrich's Lean~4 \cite{MU21}, the mathlib Community's
library \cite{Mathlib20}, Wetzler, Heule and Hunt's \texttt{drat-trim}
\cite{WHH14} for conversion to Cruz-Filipe et al.'s LRAT format
\cite{CHH+17}, and Tan, Heule and Myreen's \texttt{cake\_lpr}
\cite{THM21} for the separate checks.

The proof uses Boolean satisfiability certificates and Lean;
Section~\ref{sec:verification} describes this dependence.
The proofs, the Lean formalization, the search code, the certificate checkers and the text were produced by AI models (from OpenAI and Anthropic), working under the author's direction.
The proof is computer-assisted and has not been reviewed by human experts.

\section{Proof overview}
The lower bound is the $K_4$ blow-up in Figure~\ref{fig:lower}: every
monochromatic component has at most nine vertices. For the upper bound,
we first re-derive Dybizbański, Dzido and Radziszowski's bound
\cite[Theorem~8]{DDR16} by successive Boolean satisfiability refutations,
starting with $P_3$ on $K_5$ and ending with $P_9$ on $K_{17}$.
Each formula fixes only the monochromatic path guaranteed by the
preceding bound.

A hypothetical coloring of $K_{18}$ without a monochromatic $P_{10}$
therefore contains a $P_9$. After fixing that path, we choose a least
coloring among those obtained by the permitted vertex and color
permutations. It satisfies all the imposed lexicographic inequalities
simultaneously. To make the final refutations tractable, we divide the
remaining assignments into cubes, conjunctions of edge-color assignments.
One refutation excludes assignments outside all the cubes; a refutation
for each cube then completes the upper bound. Lean checks the reductions
and these finite certificates, as described in
Section~\ref{sec:verification}.

\section{The lower bound}\label{sec:lower}
The following $K_4$ blow-up gives a coloring of $K_{17}$ without a
monochromatic ten-vertex path. In the construction and the upper-bound
encoding we use the color labels $0,1,2$ in place of $1,2,3$. Partition $\{0,\dots,16\}$ into $A=\{0,\dots,4\}$, $B=\{5,\dots,8\}$,
$C=\{9,\dots,12\}$ and $D=\{13,\dots,16\}$. Color an edge with color $0$
if both ends lie in $A\cup B$ or both lie in $C\cup D$; with color $1$ if
it joins $A$ to $C$ or $B$ to $D$; and with color $2$ if it joins $A$ to
$D$ or $B$ to $C$. This is the blow-up of the factorization of $K_4$ into
the matchings $\{AB,CD\}$, $\{AC,BD\}$, $\{AD,BC\}$, with the edges inside
the parts given color $0$. This is the construction of Gyárfás,
Ruszinkó, Sárközy and Szemerédi \cite{GRSS07}, also presented by
Dybizbański, Dzido and Radziszowski \cite{DDR16}; both specify the odd
and even part sizes.

For each color, its \emph{label classes} are the two parts of the
corresponding partition: $(A\cup B,C\cup D)$ for color $0$,
$(A\cup C,B\cup D)$ for color $1$, and $(A\cup D,B\cup C)$ for color $2$.
The \emph{edge invariant} says that an edge of a given color has both
ends in one of its label classes. Figure~\ref{fig:lower} displays the
four constituent parts.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.42\textwidth]{../data/lower-bound.pdf}
\caption{The $K_{17}$ coloring, compressed into four parts. Each line
represents all edges between its parts; all edges within a part have
color~0. In each color, the two label classes have sizes 9 and 8.}
\label{fig:lower}
\end{figure}


\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower}
This coloring of $K_{17}$ has no monochromatic $P_{10}$. Hence
$\R(P_{10})\ge 18$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each color has two label classes: $A\cup B$ and $C\cup D$ for color $0$,
$A\cup C$ and $B\cup D$ for color $1$, $A\cup D$ and $B\cup C$ for color
$2$, of sizes $9$ and $8$ in each case. The edge invariant is that every
edge of a color lies inside one of that color's two label classes; no edge
of that color joins them. So every connected piece of a color class lies
inside one label class and has at most $9$ vertices, and a monochromatic
path, being connected, has at most $9$ vertices.
\end{proof}

\section{The upper bound}\label{sec:encoding}
We encode a hypothetical coloring without a monochromatic path and
refute the resulting finite formulas. Throughout, a \emph{coloring} of $K_n$ is a map $X$ from the edges to
$\{0,1,2\}$. For a fixed path order $m$, such a coloring is \emph{good} if it has no
monochromatic $P_m$; goodness refers to this same $m$ throughout each
formula and its associated lemmas. We order the edges $\{i,j\}$, $i<j$, row by row:
$\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\dots,\{0,n-1\},\{1,2\},\dots,\{n-2,n-1\}$, and number
them $e=0,1,\dots,\binom n2-1$ in this order.

\subsection{Variables and the coloring clauses}
We express the coloring and path conditions as Boolean clauses.
A Boolean literal is a variable or its negation; a clause is a
disjunction of literals. A model assigns truth values satisfying every
clause; a refutation certifies that no such assignment exists.

The variable $x_{e,c}=3e+c+1$ means ``edge $e$ has color $c$''.
An unordered pair in the edge subscript denotes its number in the preceding edge order. For each
edge there is one clause $x_{e,0}\vee x_{e,1}\vee x_{e,2}$ and three
clauses $\neg x_{e,c}\vee\neg x_{e,d}$, $c<d$, so that models correspond to
colorings. For a color $c$ and distinct vertices $p_0,\dots,p_{m-1}$, the
\emph{path clause}
\[
\bigvee_{i=0}^{m-2}\neg x_{\{p_i,p_{i+1}\},c}
\]
says that this path is not monochromatic in color $c$. A coloring is good
exactly when its assignment satisfies every path clause. Only the path clauses used in a refutation need to be listed: a good
coloring satisfies every path clause, hence any listed subset. Lean
rebuilds each listed clause from its color and distinct vertices, so the
solver's path-finding procedure need not be assumed complete.

\subsection{The fixed path}
Vertex permutations $\pi\in S_n$ and color permutations $\sigma\in S_3$ act
on colorings by $(g\cdot X)(\{\pi a,\pi b\})=\sigma(X(\{a,b\}))$ for
$g=(\pi,\sigma)$. On edges, $\pi\{a,b\}=\{\pi(a),\pi(b)\}$. They map good colorings to good colorings.

The following lemma fixes only the edges of the guaranteed path.

\begin{lemma}[fixed path]\label{lem:fix}
Let $k\le n$ and suppose every $3$-coloring of $K_n$ contains a
monochromatic $P_k$. Then every good coloring of $K_n$ is equivalent under
$S_n\times S_3$ to a good coloring $X$ with $X(\{t,t+1\})=0$ for
$0\le t\le k-2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take a monochromatic path $v_0,\dots,v_{k-1}$ in color $c$; apply a vertex
permutation sending $v_t$ to $t$ and the transposition of colors $c$ and
$0$.
\end{proof}

The formula for $P_m$ on $K_n$ with a fixed $P_k$ contains
the $k-1$ unit clauses $x_{\{t,t+1\},0}$. Only the path edges are
constrained; the chords of the fixed path are free.

\subsection{Symmetry constraints}
The following constraints retain a lexicographically least coloring.
Compare colorings by the lexicographic order $\lex$ of their color vectors
$(X(e))_{e}$ in edge order, with $0<1<2$. Let $\Gamma$ be a list of
generators $g=(\pi,\sigma)$. With a fixed $P_k$ on $0,\dots,k-1$ and
$W=\{k,\dots,n-1\}$, $\Gamma$ consists of the transpositions of pairs of
vertices of $W$ (in lexicographic order), the reversal $t\mapsto k-1-t$ of
$\{0,\dots,k-1\}$, and the color transposition $1\leftrightarrow2$. For $K_5$, nothing is fixed and $\Gamma$ consists of all vertex
transpositions and the color transpositions $0\leftrightarrow1$ and
$1\leftrightarrow2$.

The orbit of a coloring consists of the colorings reachable by finitely
many moves from $\Gamma$. A least member of an orbit gives simultaneous
symmetry inequalities, as follows.

\begin{lemma}[lex-leader]\label{lem:lex}
Let $S$ be the set of good colorings with color $0$ on the fixed path.
Each $g\in\Gamma$ maps $S$ to itself. Consequently, if $S$ is nonempty it
contains a coloring $X^*$ with $X^*\lex g\cdot X^*$ for every
$g\in\Gamma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
When a path is fixed, each vertex permutation in $\Gamma$ maps the
path's edge set to itself and each color permutation in $\Gamma$ fixes
color $0$, so $\Gamma$ preserves the fixed path; every $g$ preserves
goodness. (With nothing fixed, $S$ is the set of all good colorings.) Take $X^*$ to be the $\lex$-least coloring reachable from a
member of $S$ by finitely many moves from $\Gamma$; it lies in $S$, and
$g\cdot X^*$ is reachable too, so $X^*\lex g\cdot X^*$.
\end{proof}

The point of Lemma~\ref{lem:lex} is that the lex-leader constraints for
\emph{every} generator hold simultaneously for one coloring that also has
the fixed path; the two reductions are therefore jointly sound. The
generators do not need to generate the full stabilizer of the fixed path:
a weaker set of constraints only leaves more symmetry in the search.

The base formula consists of the coloring clauses, the fixed-path unit
clauses, and the clauses encoding these symmetry inequalities.
\subsection{Encoding the symmetry inequalities}\label{sec:auxiliary}
The constraint $X\lex g\cdot X$ is encoded in the standard
way~\cite[Proposition~3.1]{CGLR96}. The clauses below adapt that Boolean-variable construction to three colors. The positions compared are the edges $f$ in edge order,
except those with $\pi^{-1}(f)=f$ and $\sigma$ the identity (where
$X(f)=(g\cdot X)(f)$ identically). For the $j$-th compared position $f_j$,
let $Y_j=\sigma(X(\pi^{-1}f_j))$ be the color of $g\cdot X$ there, and let
$q_j$ be an auxiliary variable meaning ``$X$ and $g\cdot X$ agree on the
first $j$ compared positions'' ($q_0$ is true and omitted). The clauses
are, for colors $a,b$:
\begin{align*}
&q_{j-1}\to\neg\bigl(X(f_j)=a\wedge Y_j=b\bigr)\quad(a>b), &
&q_{j-1}\wedge X(f_j)=a\wedge Y_j=a\to q_j,\\
&q_j\to q_{j-1}, &
&q_j\wedge X(f_j)=a\to Y_j=a.
\end{align*}
Under the intended meaning of the $q_j$, every clause holds exactly when
$X\lex g\cdot X$.
Its soundness is proved in Lean: assigning
the auxiliaries their intended values extends every coloring satisfying
the inequalities to a model of the encoded clauses.

\subsection{Successive path bounds}\label{sec:ladder}
The formula for $P_m$ on $K_n$ with fixed $P_k$ is the formula of Sections~4.1--4.4 with the fixed path on $0,\dots,k-1$, together
with all path clauses for $P_m$. If it is unsatisfiable and every
$3$-coloring of $K_n$ contains a monochromatic $P_k$, then by
Lemmas~\ref{lem:fix} and~\ref{lem:lex} every $3$-coloring of $K_n$
contains a monochromatic $P_m$. Every $3$-coloring of a larger complete
graph then does too, by restriction.

The sequence of bounds is
\begin{gather*}
\R(P_3)\le5,\quad \R(P_4)\le6,\quad \R(P_5)\le9,\quad
\R(P_6)\le10,\\
\R(P_7)\le13,\quad \R(P_8)\le14,\quad \R(P_9)\le17.
\end{gather*}
The first formula fixes nothing; each later formula fixes the path
obtained at the preceding stage. Each is refuted as a whole. The final
formula, for $P_{10}$ on $K_{18}$, fixes $P_9$ and is divided into cubes.
The formula sizes and smaller-order checks are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:computation}.

\subsection{Covering the cases}\label{sec:cnc}
We split the possible colorings into cases and certify completeness
separately. A cube is a conjunction of literals fixing the colors of some edges.
Unit propagation forces the last unassigned literal of a clause when
all its other literals are false. Walking through the edges in order, the
CaDiCaL solver of Biere et al.\ \cite{BFF+24} tries each color and
propagates the consequences, including path clauses, through Fazekas et
al.'s IPASIR-UP interface \cite{FNP+23,FNP+24}. Choices leading to a conflict are discarded; an edge with only
one surviving color is fixed without counting as a split. The walk stops
after ten edges with at least two surviving colors. This gives 15,316
cubes. Continuing the same procedure, with conflict rejection and unit
propagation, splits sixteen of them on four further split edges, giving
41 surviving children each and 656 in total. The final list has $15{,}316-16+656=15{,}956$ cubes.

The discarded branches are accounted for by the cover formula: it
consists of the base formula, the 162 path clauses learned during
generation, and the negation of every final cube (one clause per cube).
The computational facts used below are that this cover formula and the
formula for each cube, augmented by the path clauses used in its
refutation, are unsatisfiable. For each formula, Lean's verified LRAT
checker accepts a propositional refutation. The resulting 15,957 certificates, together
with the seven smaller-formula certificates, give 15,964 in all.
The following lemma combines the cover refutation with the cube
refutations. In this lemma $c$ denotes a cube, and $\mathcal P$ includes
every path clause for the given path order.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cover}
Let $F$ be a formula, $\mathcal C$ a list of cubes, and $\mathcal P$ the
set of all path clauses. If $F\cup\mathcal P\cup\{\neg c: c\in\mathcal C\}$
is unsatisfiable and $F\cup\mathcal P\cup c$ is unsatisfiable for every
$c\in\mathcal C$, then $F\cup\mathcal P$ is unsatisfiable.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A model of $F\cup\mathcal P$ satisfies some cube $c\in\mathcal C$, since
otherwise it falsifies a literal of every cube and so satisfies every
$\neg c$. It is then a model of $F\cup\mathcal P\cup c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Suppose a $3$-coloring $X$ of $K_{18}$ has no monochromatic $P_{10}$. Since
$\R(P_9)\le17$ by the successive bounds above, $X$ contains a
monochromatic $P_9$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:fix} a vertex permutation and a
color permutation carry $X$ to a good coloring with color $0$ on the path
$0,1,\dots,8$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:lex} its orbit under the generators
contains a good coloring $X^*$ that keeps the fixed path and satisfies
every lex-leader inequality.

With the auxiliaries set to their meaning,
$X^*$ satisfies the base formula and every path clause. By the cover
certificate, $X^*$ satisfies some cube of the final list (otherwise it
would satisfy the cover formula), and that cube's formula is unsatisfiable
by its own certificate: a contradiction.
Thus $\R(P_{10})\le18$, while Proposition~\ref{prop:lower} gives
$\R(P_{10})\ge18$.
\end{proof}

\section{Computational verification}\label{sec:verification}
The Lean development proves the following formal statement:
\[
\texttt{R3P10.R3\_P10\_eq\_18}\;:\quad\texttt{ramsey3Path 10 = 18}.
\]
Its colorings assign one of the colors $0,1,2$ to every unordered pair of
vertices, including unused diagonal pairs. Relabeling the colors as
$1,2,3$ gives the convention of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
A formal monochromatic path is an injective map from $\{0,\dots,m-1\}$
whose consecutive pairs all have one color, with no condition on the
other pairs. Thus it is the same non-induced path on $m$ vertices used
here. The formal Ramsey number is the natural-number infimum of the
orders at which every coloring contains such a path. The proof shows
that this set contains $18$ and excludes every smaller order by
restricting the countercoloring on $17$ vertices. The final theorem has
no certificate hypotheses; the finite coloring in its lower bound is
exactly the construction of Section~\ref{sec:lower}.

Lean proves the reductions, the lower bound and the soundness of the
certificate checker. The finite certificate checks are evaluated by compiled code, so their
validity also relies on faithful compiled evaluation. The checking notes
specify the logical assumptions and reproduction instructions.
The refutations were checked twice, using a total of about twenty-three
days of processor time. The archived certificates \cite{Gra26} permit
checking without repeating the search.

\subsection{Certificate sizes}
The initial cubes have $11$ to $20$ literals; the sixteen selected parents
have $11$ literals each. Generation of the initial cubes and their
children uses $18$ and $144$ path clauses respectively, for a total of
$162$. The cover formula has $30{,}790$ clauses: the $14{,}672$ base
clauses, these $162$ path clauses, and one negated clause for each of the
$15{,}956$ cubes.
