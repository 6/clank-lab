# Clank Lab

Proposed solutions to open problems in math.

Not yet reviewed by experts. Corrections are welcome as pull requests or issues.

Produced by AI models under the author's direction.

Each result is labeled with how it is checked. **Proved in Lean** means the Lean proof assistant checks the main theorem from the definitions; the others are computer-assisted or written proofs, often with parts checked in Lean.

## Results on conjectures and questions

Proofs and counterexamples for published conjectures and questions.

| Result | Open question | Published |
|---|---|---|
| [**The second-order asymptotic of the EKG sequence**](results/ekg-sequence/)<br>*Proved in Lean* | The second-order growth $a(n) = n + n/(3\log n) + o(n/\log n)$ of the [EKG sequence](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/EKGSequence.html) at almost all $n$, conjectured by Lagarias, Rains and Sloane in 2002. Building on [Tomer Haver's 2026 reduction and upper bound](https://zenodo.org/records/23129632), we prove it for the smoothed sequence (values $p$ and $3p$ replaced by $2p$) and settle OEIS conjectures that follow. | October 20, 2026 |
| [**The convex uniform 5-polytopes: a computer-assisted classification**](results/uniform-5-polytopes/)<br>*Partly in Lean* | Whether the known convex [uniform 5-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_5-polytope) are all of them. Conway and Guy announced the four-dimensional list in 1965, and [Möller gave a complete proof in 2004](https://ediss.sub.uni-hamburg.de/handle/ediss/689); five was listed as open by Schulte (2017) and Winter (2021). We give a computer-assisted proof that the known list is complete. | October 20, 2026 |
| [**A planar graph without four- or nine-cycles that is not three-colorable**](results/steinberg-c4-c9/)<br>*Partly in Lean* | Whether every planar graph with no 4- or 9-cycles is [3-colorable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_coloring), asked by Lu and coauthors in 2009. We give a computer-assisted proof that one isn't: an 82-vertex counterexample. | October 20, 2026 |
| [**A counterexample to Stanley's conjecture on Smith forms of Jucys–Murphy elements**](results/stanley-smith-forms/)<br>*Written proof, partly in Lean* | Whether the Smith normal form of each [Jucys–Murphy element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jucys%E2%80%93Murphy_element) acting on an integral Specht module is given by products of contents, as conjectured by Richard Stanley in 2016. We disprove it with a written proof, with Lean and computer checks; the smallest counterexamples have shape (3,1,1). | October 21, 2026 |
| [**Smith normal form of the n-cycle class sum**](results/stanley-class-sum-snf/)<br>*Proved in Lean* | The [Smith normal form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_normal_form) of multiplication by the sum of all $n$-cycles in the center of $\mathbb{Z}S_n$, asked by Richard Stanley in [2019](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/335226). We give the invariant factors for every $n$, proving his two conjectured special cases. | October 21, 2026 |
| [**Sharp linear bounds for the EKG sequence**](results/ekg-bounds/)<br>*Partly in Lean* | Whether the [EKG sequence](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/EKGSequence.html) always satisfies $13n/28 \le a(n) \le 12n/7$, with equality only at $n = 28$ and $n = 7$, as Lagarias, Rains and Sloane conjectured in 2002; they proved $n/260 \le a(n) \le 14n$. We give a computer-assisted proof of the conjecture. | October 22, 2026 |
| [**Existence of locally toroidal regular 4-polytopes**](results/locally-toroidal-polytopes/)<br>*Proved in Lean* | [Schulte–Weiss Problem 16 (2006)](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608397) asks to classify these [regular polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_regular_polytope) for distinct odd parameters. Only $(3,5)$ and its dual were previously known. We prove the existence part, conjectured by McMullen–Schulte in 2002; finiteness of the universal polytopes and group classification remain open. | October 22, 2026 |
| [**Locked tromino tilings of rectangles**](results/locked-trominoes/)<br>*Proved in Lean* | Which rectangles have a locked [tromino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyomino) tiling, in which no two tiles can be re-cut into two other trominoes. Tucker-Foltz (2023) excluded sides of at most 3, built them for $3 \mid m$, $m \ge 6$, $n \ge 100$, and conjectured that $n \ge 4$ suffices. We prove the conjecture, completing the classification. | October 22, 2026 |
| [**A generalized pseudostandard prefix with no string attractor of size four**](results/pseudostandard-attractors/)<br>*Computer-assisted* | Whether every pseudopalindromic prefix of a binary generalized pseudostandard sequence has a [string attractor](https://doi.org/10.1145/3188745.3188814) of at most four positions, as conjectured by Dvořáková and Hendrychová in [August 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00850v1); smaller bounds were known for three subclasses. We give a computer-assisted proof that it fails at length 36. | October 22, 2026 |
| [**A conjecture of Elgin, Reading and Stella on rank-two scattering coefficients**](results/scattering-coefficients/)<br>*Proved in Lean* | Whether the coefficients $\tau^{1,5}(2j,j)$ of a rank-two [cluster scattering diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_algebra) are given by a binomial sum, as Elgin, Reading and Stella conjectured in 2024. [Burcroff, Lee, Mou, Musiker and Reineke](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14672) proved most of their conjectures in 2025 and listed this one as unsolved. We prove it for every $j \ge 1$, in the convention of the conjecture's table and code. | October 22, 2026 |
| [**A monotone counterexample to a question on distributional complexity**](results/monotone-query-gap/)<br>*Partly in Lean* | Whether average query and subcube partition costs agree for every monotone [Boolean function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_function) at every coin bias, asked by Köhler-Schindler and Steif in [2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12995). Their nonmonotone ALL-EQUAL and four-input $h$ examples have strict gaps (arXiv v1, Example 2.4 and Section 2.3). We answer no with an explicit five-input example at fair coins. | October 22, 2026 |
| [**Fathi's consecutive-integers bound does not extend to multitwists**](results/multitwist-exceptions/)<br>*Written proof, partly in Lean* | Whether Fathi's consecutive-exponent bound extends to multitwists, asked by [Ivanov in 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608325), after Fathi's single-twist theorem. On the five-holed sphere we give a written counterexample with a fixed positive two-component multitwist and varying pseudo-Anosov bases, whose exceptional exponents are arbitrarily far apart. The weaker count question remains open. | October 22, 2026 |
| [**A counterexample to a lit-only sigma-game conjecture**](results/lit-only-sigma/)<br>*Proved in Lean* | Whether a connected graph's same-start light-number gap exceeding half its order forces a complete multipartite graph, as [Goldwasser, Wang and Wu conjectured in 2011](https://doi.org/10.37236/701), motivated by their multipartite examples. We give computer-assisted counterexamples on $7m$ vertices for every $m\geq1$, with same-start gap at least $4m$. | October 23, 2026 |
| [**Strongly cospectral sets in seven-dimensional cubelike graphs**](results/cubelike-cospectrality/)<br>*Partly in Lean* | Whether Árnadóttir and Godsil's bound for strongly cospectral sets in [cubelike graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley_graph) is tight in every dimension ([2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07568)); they attained it in dimensions three through six. We prove that the seven-dimensional maximum is four, rather than their bound of eight, answering no. | October 23, 2026 |
| [**Greedy play can require more colors in the coloring game**](results/game-grundy-gap/)<br>*Computer-assisted* | Whether the game Grundy number is always at most the [game chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_chromatic_number), asked by Havet and Zhu in [June 2011](https://inria.hal.science/inria-00600738v1/document) and left open by Hollom (2023). The same counterexample now has the exact values $\chi_g(G)=5$ and $\Gamma_g(G)=6$. Arbitrarily large gaps remain open. | October 23, 2026 |
| [**The weak parking-space conjecture**](results/weak-parking/)<br>*Computer-assisted* | Armstrong, Reiner and Rhoades's [2012 conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.1760) asks whether two parking-space representations of a finite [reflection group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_group), including their cyclic symmetry, agree. Every irreducible real type except $E_7$ and $E_8$ was known. We give a computer-assisted proof for these last two types, completing the weak conjecture. | October 23, 2026 |
| [**The strong Lefschetz property for complex reflection coinvariants**](results/coinvariant-lefschetz/)<br>*Computer-assisted* | Whether every irreducible [complex reflection group's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_reflection_group) coinvariant ring has the strong Lefschetz property, asked by Wachi in [2013](https://doi.org/10.34508/repsympo.2013.0_140). Previous results covered every type except $G_{34}$. We give a computer-assisted proof of that remaining case, completing the general assertion over $\mathbb{C}$. | October 23, 2026 |
| [**Symmetric chains in noncrossing partition lattices**](results/noncrossing-symmetric-chains/)<br>*Partly in Lean* | Whether every well-generated complex reflection group's [noncrossing partition lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncrossing_partition) has a symmetric chain decomposition, asked by [Mühle in 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06942v1). Earlier results covered the infinite families and most exceptional types. We give a computer-assisted proof for the remaining types, completing the chain assertion. We do not settle the stronger Boolean question. | October 23, 2026 |

| Result | Open question | Published |

| [**The seventh ternary density Hales–Jewett number**](results/density-seven/)<br>*Computer-assisted* | The largest set of seven-letter words over three letters with no combinatorial line (the seventh [density Hales–Jewett number](https://oeis.org/A156989)). D.H.J. Polymath knew the values through six and the 1,302 lower bound by [March 2009](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2009/03/04/dhj3-900-999-density-hales-jewett-type-numbers/), later tentatively conjecturing equality in [their 2010 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.0374). We give a computer-assisted proof that it is. | October 20, 2026 |
| [**Nonexistence of (243,121,60) difference sets in C3 × C9 × C9**](results/difference-set-243/)<br>*Partly in Lean* | Whether a (243,121,60) [difference set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_set) exists in $C_3 \times C_9 \times C_9$, recorded as unresolved in Vera López and García Sánchez's 1997 table and listed in [Gordon's repository](https://dmgordon.org/difference-sets/). We give a computer-assisted proof that none does. | October 21, 2026 |
| [**The three-color Ramsey number of the 10-vertex path**](results/path-ramsey/)<br>*Proved in Lean, with compiled checking* | The three-color [Ramsey number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey%27s_theorem) $R(P_{10}, P_{10}, P_{10})$ of the [path](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/PathGraph.html) on 10 vertices, suggested by Faudree–Schelp (1975) to be 18 and [stated as a conjecture by Dybizbański–Dzido–Radziszowski (2012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.5851). Paths up to 9 vertices were settled by 2016, leaving 10 as the first open case in [Radziszowski's survey](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/DS1). We give a computer-assisted proof that it is 18. | October 21, 2026 |
| [**The Thomson problem for N = 10**](results/thomson-10/)<br>*Proved in Lean* | The lowest-energy arrangement of N = 10 charges on a sphere, the next open case of the [Thomson problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_problem) (1904) after an AI-generated, not yet reviewed Lean proof for N = 9 was posted in October 2026. We give a computer-assisted proof, checked in Lean, that the bicapped square antiprism is the only minimizer up to orthogonal maps and relabeling. | October 22, 2026 |
| [**Nonexistence of Williamson matrices of order 65**](results/williamson-order-65/)<br>*Computer-assisted* | Whether [Williamson matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamson_conjecture) exist at order 65, described by [Bright in 2019](https://cs.curtisbright.com/extended-research-statement.pdf) as “currently the smallest order for which it is unknown” whether they exist; 67 remains open. A computer-assisted search finds none. The CPU search is replayable, with receipts and two coverage and accounting checkers. | October 22, 2026 |
