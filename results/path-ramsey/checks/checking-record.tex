\section{Checks independent of Lean}\label{sec:separate}

The result was also checked by a separate route that does not use
Lean.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Certificates.} CaDiCaL wrote a DRAT proof for each rung and
each cube; drat-trim~\cite{WHH14} converted it to LRAT~\cite{CHH+17}, and
\texttt{cake\_lpr}~\cite{THM21}, a checker verified in CakeML down to its
machine code, accepted every rung, every one of the $15{,}956$ cubes and
the cover formula.
\item \emph{Inputs.} For each check, an input checker confirmed that the
file given to \texttt{cake\_lpr} is exactly the base formula,
plus the solver's clause log, plus the cube's unit clauses (or the negated
cubes, for the cover).
\item \emph{Path clauses.} A separate checker, sharing no code with the
encoder or the solver, confirmed that every logged clause consists of
$m-1$ distinct negative literals of one color whose edges form a path on
$m$ distinct vertices. It rejects ten kinds of planted corruption.
\item \emph{Replays.} The solver was deterministic under the pinned build. Every cube was solved a
second time on a different computer from the one that first solved it,
with byte-identical proofs and clause logs; re-solving all cubes again to
produce the Lean certificates reproduced every proof and log once more.
\item \emph{Encoding audit.} An independently written auditor, which does
not import the encoder, checks every clause of every rung's base formula
against a direct reading of the problem (with the auxiliaries at their
meaning), checks that the clause multiset is exactly the one the stated
encoding generates, with nothing missing or extra, checks that every
generator preserves the fixed path, checks the reductions exhaustively on
$K_4$ and $K_5$ (where the formula accepts exactly the colorings it should
and no class of good colorings is lost), and rejects $30$ planted
corruptions of the formulas, logs and reductions.
\item \emph{Independent re-check.} A separate verification, with its own
code and not importing the search or the Lean development, reconstructed
the cube list and the inputs of every one of the $15{,}956$ cubes and of
the cover from the frozen data, rechecked the path clauses, and checked
every piece's refutation again; the cover refutation also passed an
independently written LRUP checker, and both checkers rejected a corrupted
proof and a corrupted input.
\item \emph{Lower bound.} An exhaustive search confirms that the $K_{17}$
coloring has no monochromatic $P_{10}$ (and that it has a monochromatic
$P_9$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Computation and replay}\label{sec:replay}

\subsection{Cost}
The search took about $217$ CPU-hours of solving, $48$ of checking and
converting the proofs with drat-trim, and $3$ of checking them with
\texttt{cake\_lpr}; the hardest single cube took about
$2.9$ hours to solve. We ran the full check of
Section~\ref{sec:commands} twice, in independent pieces. In the first run,
which produced the Lean certificates, re-solving every cube took about
$185$ CPU-hours, conversion to LRAT with drat-trim about $45$, packing the
certificates about $3$ and the Lean checks about $15$: $249$ in all. In
the second, recorded in the accompanying \path{COMPLETION.md}, the same
steps took about $229$, $50$, $5$ and $18$: $301$ in all. The README of the
certificate dataset~\cite{Gra26} records the same two runs. The DRAT
proofs of the cubes total about $58$\,GB and are not distributed: the
solver is deterministic, and the replay regenerates them.

\subsection{How to check}\label{sec:commands}
The accompanying files contain the Lean project in \path{lean/}, the
certificate checkers in \path{checks/}, and the formulas and cube lists in
\path{data/}. They pin the Lean toolchain and Mathlib, and the commits of
CaDiCaL, drat-trim and \texttt{cake\_lpr} that the checks build. The Lean
project is checked with the standard commands:
\begin{Verbatim}
cd lean
lake exe cache get
lake build
\end{Verbatim}
This builds the encoding proof, the conditional theorems, the lower bound
and the statement, and checks their axioms; it needs no generated
certificate data and does not by itself establish the value. The
computation is replayed with
\begin{Verbatim}
python3 checks/check.py quick
python3 checks/check.py full
\end{Verbatim}
The quick mode re-solves the ladder and checks it, the cover and a random
sample of cubes both in Lean and with \texttt{cake\_lpr}, and runs the
planted errors; it prints the seed of its sample. The full mode re-solves
and checks every cube in Lean ($249$ and $301$ CPU-hours in our two runs, in independent
pieces that can run in parallel on several machines), then the ladder and
the cover, and finally runs \texttt{lake build R3P10Final}, which proves
\texttt{R3\_P10\_eq\_18} and prints its axioms as in
Section~\ref{sec:lean}. With \texttt{--cross-check} it also checks every
cube with the path-clause checker, the input checker and
\texttt{cake\_lpr}. The certificates can instead be installed from the
published dataset~\cite{Gra26}, which holds the $15{,}956$ compact cube
certificates and an installer; the full mode then skips the search and
conversion for every cube ($234$ and $284$ CPU-hours of our two runs) and
goes straight to the Lean checks.
