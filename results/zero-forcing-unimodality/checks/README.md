# Checking the zero-forcing polynomial

The single entry point is `python3 -I checks/check.py` from the result directory. Python 3.10 or newer and its standard library suffice; `requirements.txt` records that no packages are needed. The entry point launches each checker in a separate interpreter. None imports a search program or another checker.

`data/certificate.json` gives the 73 vertices, the explicit edge list, six contiguous clique modules, 64 complement patterns and the full coefficient vector. Modules are vertices 0–31, 32–64, 65–66, 67–68, 69–70 and 71–72. An edge joins every pair in a module and every pair with one vertex in a left module and one in a right module. No other edges occur.

* `checker_a.py` checks the graph and pair forts, performs ordinary forcing on one representative of every binary module pattern, and counts its exact multiplicity.
* Independently written `checker_b.py` checks the graph and every within-module pair fort, then enumerates all 90,882 labeled complements containing at most one vertex from each module. It performs vertex-level forcing for each, without using orbit multiplicities or quotient forcing rules. Exactly 5,362 starting sets succeed.
* Separately written `checker_c.py` checks the coverage argument. It enumerates 90,882 module-count vectors: 64 binary patterns and 90,818 vectors excluded by a pair fort. Their binomial weights sum to all 9,444,732,965,739,290,427,392 subsets. The total is the binomial identity for the six module sizes and checks enumeration completeness, not the graph. The substantive check is that each of the 90,818 non-binary vectors is excluded by a checked within-module pair fort. This checker validates coverage, not forcing labels or coefficients.

The two appearances of 90,882 count different objects: labeled binary complements for B, and unrestricted module-count vectors for C. A and B validate the polynomial independently. C supplies a separate check that the exclusion and enumeration cover every starting set. All arithmetic is exact; there is no probabilistic sampling. Correctness still relies on the Python implementations and runtime and on the mathematical interpretation of a fort, explained in the paper. There is no Lean formalization.

The entry point also checks the complete graph on six vertices, the four-vertex path and the four-cycle, whose coefficient lists are respectively `[0,0,0,0,0,6,1]`, `[0,2,6,4,1]` and `[0,0,4,4,1]`. It demands the specified rejection reasons for the four altered inputs in `data/corruptions/`: a wrong coefficient, a missing clique edge, extra edges between the two left cliques, and a missing binary pattern. A and B reject the first three; C rejects the missing-pattern cover. The extra-edge test uses generic audit mode so that it reaches the forcing calculation rather than stopping at the graph's identity. Ordinary acceptance always uses the fixed graph.

The paper can be built with a standard TeX Live installation providing `amsmath`, `amsthm`, `geometry`, `tikz` and `hyperref`. Run `pdflatex paper.tex` twice in `paper/`; auxiliary files are not part of the distributed bundle.

## Further credits

[Caleb C. Fast and Illya V. Hicks](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2018.05.002), *Effects of vertex degrees on the zero-forcing number and propagation time of a graph*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 250 (2018), 215–226, introduced forts and their obstruction to forcing; Wang, Proposition 2.3, states the transversal form. The paper attributes its specialization of Wang’s Lemma 3.5 at the lemma itself.

The main sources are [Boyer et al., arXiv 1801.08910v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08910v1), Section 6, Conjecture 1 (Question 1 in the [published article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2018.11.033)), and [Xinjun Wang, *Modular Compression of Leaky Forts and Leaky Forcing Polynomials*](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20388397), Lemma 3.5 and Corollary 3.6, May 26, 2026. The former's Theorem 16 gives initial-half monotonicity; the latter includes ordinary forcing at leak number zero and lists unimodality in Section 7(3) as a future direction.

[Bonnie Jacob, *The Zero Forcing Span of a Graph*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06234v1), Section 3.1.2, Proposition 13, treats polynomial information in the study of span. [Krishna Menon and Anurag Singh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01423v1), Conjecture 1.1 and the graph-operation results, and [Samuel German](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10836v1), Theorem 1 in Section 3 (Theorem 3.1 in the arXiv HTML rendering) and Corollary 2, address the separate coefficientwise path-extremality question. Their preprints do not exhibit this graph or a universal unimodality answer. German's Section 3, Lemma 7 also uses twin-pair fort obstructions.

[Sidoine's *Separator algebras and transfer theory for the zero-forcing polynomial*](https://github.com/gsidoine/zero-forcing-separator-algebras/tree/2df83a43f9476a3b965135dd6b076838d74451ff), October 6, 2026, describes separator formulas and transfer algorithms and discloses AI assistance. Its inspected abstract, introduction and discussion do not claim a unimodality settlement. Its algorithms are not used here. [Makowsky and Rakita](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00268v4), Examples 1.10(ii) and Conjecture 1, discuss almost-all-graphs unimodality for augmented properties; one counterexample to universal unimodality does not settle that question.

## Source access

As of November 1, 2026, the result is new as far as we could check. Seven related publications were read only in part, for the reasons given with each. Items 1, 2 and 6 are behind publisher paywalls. Items 3, 4 and 5 are free to read on ScienceDirect in a web browser, but ScienceDirect refuses scripted downloads, so we read only the parts listed. Searches of arXiv, open-access indexes, author pages, institutional repositories, conference pages and web archives found no other full copy. What we read shows that each studies a different question:

1. Brimkov–Mikesell–Hicks, [*Improved Computational Approaches and Heuristics for Zero Forcing*](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.2020.1032) (2021). The abstract and zbMATH record were read, together with two of its authors' [2024 survey](https://doi.org/10.1090/noti2874). These describe Boolean-satisfiability and heuristic computation of the minimum zero-forcing number, and polyhedral facets: "In [BMH21] it was shown how the facets of the zero forcing polyhedron corresponding to certain commonly occurring forts in the graphs can be characterized." Nothing counts zero-forcing sets by size.
2. Karbasioun–Ameri, [*Zero forcing number of fuzzy graphs with application*](https://doi.org/10.3233/JIFS-192201) (2020). The abstract and keywords were read. The paper gives bounds and an algorithm for the zero-forcing number of fuzzy graphs, with medical applications. Its one passage citing Boyer and coauthors, as indexed by Semantic Scholar, recalls the polynomial's definition.
3. Karst–Shen–Troxell–Vu, [*Blocking zero forcing processes in Cartesian products of graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2020.06.002) (2020). The publisher's preview was read: abstract, introduction, section headings and references. Every section bounds the zero blocking number, the minimum size of a fort, of a Cartesian product. Boyer and coauthors appear only in a list of recent zero-forcing articles.
4. Beaudouin-Lafon et al., [*On the zero blocking number of rectangular, cylindrical, and Möbius grids*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2019.11.015) (2019/2020). The same preview material was read, and it shows the same scope for grids.
5. Menon–Singh, [*Exploring the influence of graph operations on zero forcing sets*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2025.114516) (2025). The [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01423v1) was read in full. The final article's abstract matches it apart from two words, and its 29 deposited references are the preprint's, in the same order. The subject is domination by the path polynomial (Question 2 of Boyer and coauthors), not unimodality.
6. German, [*The Path-Extremal Conjecture for Zero Forcing: Distance-Hereditary Graphs and a Split-Decomposition Reduction*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-92-3309-0_31), COCOON 2026, proceedings volume dated 2027. The [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10836v1) was read in full. The chapter's abstract, keywords, first two pages and 15 references match it. Unimodality appears only as a question of Boyer and coauthors.
7. Iain Beaton, [*When are Graph Polynomials Unimodal?*](https://www.upei.ca/notice/2026/07/smcs-research-seminar-when-are-graph-polynomials-unimodal), July 16, 2026. No slides, recording or paper was found. The abstract concerns when polynomials generated by clutters are unimodal and announces no counterexample. [Ben Cameron's problem page](https://www.smcs.upei.ca/~brcameron/graphpolys.html), at the host department and last modified October 15, 2026, still asks whether the zero-forcing polynomial is always unimodal.

These are judgments from partial texts, not full readings. Citation indexes and the inspected sources cannot establish that no earlier answer exists anywhere.
