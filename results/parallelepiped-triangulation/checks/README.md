# Checking notes

The single entry point is `python3 -I checks/check.py`, run from the result's directory. It needs Python 3.12 or newer, numpy and scipy (pinned in `requirements.txt` by version and file hashes), and a C++17 compiler (`c++`, or the one named by `CXX`). It runs each program below as a separate process, prints a PASS line for each check, and stops with exit status 1 at the first failure. `--list` lists the 24 checks and `--part K/N` runs every N-th of them, starting with the K-th.

## The data

- `data/B.json`: the generators of $P$ as the columns of a 4×4 matrix.
- `data/cover.txt`: the subtraction tree of Theorem 1.3. Its format is described in `check_cover.py`.
- `data/fans.jsonl`: the vertex fans of Proposition 1.5, one line per vertex $v$ of $P$, each cone given by the four lattice points $p$ of $P$ whose differences $p - v$ span it.
- `data/classes/n4_D<n>.jsonl`, for $n = 2, \dots, 8$: one line per class of 4-dimensional lattice parallelepipeds of determinant $n$, with a generator matrix `B` (columns) and either a unimodular triangulation (`TRIANGULATION`, its simplices by vertex coordinates) or the status `UNSAT`, for the classes without one.

The deletion orders (Section 5 of the paper) are not stored: `prune.py` regenerates each in a few seconds, and the checkers then read it.

## The programs

- `check_hand_proof.py` (standard library only) reads Tables 1–3 from `paper/paper.tex` and checks every finite statement of the proof of Theorem 1.1 by brute force: the 20 lattice points of $P' = P[1,2,2,5]$, that each sum in Step 1 is the sum of exactly one pair of lattice points of $P'$, that Tables 2 and 3 list every weighted set of Lemma 4.1 exactly once, and every identity, including that some point with positive weight on the left lies outside the known face on the right, and that the known face is a lattice point, a Step 1 edge or the Step 2 tetrahedron. It is run on two corrupted copies of the source (a wrong identity, a deleted row), which it must reject.
- `prune.py` produces a deletion order (Section 5, "A second proof"). `check_prune.py` shares no code with it: it recomputes from the generators, in exact rational arithmetic, the lattice points and every unimodular simplex of the parallelepiped, and accepts only if the order deletes each simplex exactly once, each time across a facet not contained in a facet of the parallelepiped whose every unimodular partner on the other side was deleted before. `prune_check.cpp`, written separately, checks the same certificate (converted by `prune_prep.py`). Both must reject the order with its last deletion moved first.
- `check_cover.py` replays the subtraction tree of Theorem 1.3 exactly, recomputing every simplex and every Farkas certificate, and must reject the tree with its last line removed.
- `check_classes.py` (checker A) enumerates every Hermite normal form of determinant $n$, checks that each is equivalent to exactly one listed class, and checks every listed triangulation: $24n$ distinct unimodular simplices with vertices in the parallelepiped that pairwise intersect properly, decided by `compat.cpp` through circuits. The classes without a triangulation are then refuted by deletion orders checked by both pruning checkers. `classes_check.py` (checker B, numpy and scipy), written separately, re-enumerates the Hermite normal forms with its own canonical form under signed column permutations and verifies every triangulation: for each pair of simplices, linear programming proposes a hyperplane separating them through their common vertices, which is then verified in exact rational arithmetic.
- `check_fans.py` (checker A, standard library and `compat.cpp`) and `fans_check.py` (checker B, standard library only, written separately) check `data/fans.jsonl` for Proposition 1.5 (Appendix B of the paper). Both recompute the lattice points of $P$ from its generators and check that the file has one fan for each of the 16 vertices, whose cones are distinct, unimodular and spanned by lattice points of $P - v$. Checker A decides proper intersection by circuits (`compat.cpp` on the simplices with vertices $0, p_1 - v, \dots, p_4 - v$) and covering by an exact identity of section volumes; checker B decides proper intersection by Fourier–Motzkin elimination and covering by checking that every facet not on the boundary of the vertex cone has a cone on each side. Both compute the Hilbert basis of the cone at $0$ and check the counts 56, 19, 8 and 11. Both must reject the fan at $0$ with its last cone dropped, and with its first cone replaced by another unimodular cone.
- The search that produced `data/fans.jsonl` is not run by `check.py`: for each vertex, `localfan.py` (with `enc.py --meta`, which lists the lattice points and unimodular simplices, and `proper.cpp`) asks the SAT solver CaDiCaL, built at commit `c60730422e758ef1cebe7aeddf2dda31c996bf04`, for a set of unimodular cones satisfying necessary conditions of a fan and refines it until the cones pairwise intersect properly. To rerun it, from a scratch directory inside the result's directory: `python3 -I ../checks/enc.py ../data/B.json P.cnf --points 0 --meta P.meta`, then `CADICAL=… PROPER=… python3 -I ../checks/localfan.py P.meta 1,0,0,0 --out fans.jsonl` for each vertex in the order of `data/fans.jsonl`.

For the classes, the counts are the paper's table: 15, 40, 155, 156, 600, 400 and 1395 Hermite normal forms in 4, 4, 16, 8, 26, 12 and 61 classes for determinants 2 to 8, with 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 1 and 2 classes without a unimodular triangulation, which are those of Theorem 1.4.

## Scope

The checks cover the finite statements of the proof of Theorem 1.1, the second proof of Theorem 1.1, Theorem 1.3, Theorem 1.4 and Propositions 1.5 and 1.6. Corollary 1.2 and the lemmas are proved in the paper. Correctness of the checks relies on the Python and C++ implementations, their compilers and runtimes; the SAT solver is used only by the search and is not trusted. Of the certificates, only the subtraction tree has a single checker; the class checkers are not run on corrupted inputs. There is no formal proof in a proof assistant.

The full run takes about 25 minutes (checker B checks triangulations in a pool of 12 processes; the rest runs on one core), with a peak of about 370 MB in one process. The paper builds with a standard TeX Live installation: run `pdflatex paper.tex` twice in `paper/`.

## Source access

As of November 5, 2026 we found no posted work giving a lattice parallelepiped or zonotope without a unimodular triangulation. Read in full or in the relevant parts: the survey's versions 1 to 3, the paper of Codenotti and Santos and Codenotti's thesis, the preprint of Firla and Ziegler (revised September 24, 1997), the paper of Bouvier and Gonzalez-Sprinberg, both papers of Bruns, Gubeladze and Trung, the survey of Ferroni and Higashitani (arXiv version 4), a 2006 draft of the book of Bruns and Gubeladze, and Liu's paper. Not read: von Thaden's thesis, the 1999 paper and the published book of Bruns and Gubeladze, all three cited in the survey whose authors posed the question afterwards; Sebő's paper, cited for a theorem as reported by Bruns, Gubeladze and Trung; and Blanco's chapter, of which we read the title and the zbMATH summary (Zbl 1428.52019): "In this paper I will study a 3-parameter family of lattice parallelepipeds. I will study the behavior of the lattice with respect to the parameters and prove that three 2-parameter subfamilies have unimodular covers." The paper lists these in Appendix C.
