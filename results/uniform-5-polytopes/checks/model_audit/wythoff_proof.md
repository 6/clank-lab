# R2: Coxeter constructions, faces, and exact index contracts

Scope: a mathematical construction proof for the independent `check_u3`
models. This is not a certificate for snubs or the exceptional 600-cell
subsets, and does not declare all of R2 closed. The theorem proves
uniformity before separation is used. The software contract in Section 8
must additionally be enforced to justify the returned indexed lists.
No producer construction is used as a premise.

Source anchors below refer to the pre-repair `geom.py`, `tcert.py`, and
`ktypes.py`; use function names if later edits move lines.
No computational replay is asserted by this document.

## 1. Exact objects and foundational Coxeter facts

Let the unit simple roots `alpha_i` be a basis of a Euclidean space E,
with Gram matrix

    G_ii = 1,  G_ij = -cos(pi/m_ij)  (i != j).

Use the standard finite real Coxeter system for this matrix, with
`s_i(v) = v - 2 <v,alpha_i> alpha_i` and dominant chamber
`C = {v : <v,alpha_i> >= 0 for all i}`. The systems needed here are
A3, B3, H3, A4, B4, D4, F4, H4, A5, B5, D5 and their parabolic
subsystems, precisely the diagrams in `geom.DIAGRAMS` (lines 64–75).
All are standard finite diagrams. The argument is for unit roots, including
the two root lengths of a crystallographic system normalized individually;
it does not use the crystallographic integrality condition.

The foundational finite-Coxeter facts used here are: positive roots have
nonnegative simple-root coordinates; the roots
`s_i1 ... s_i(j-1) alpha_ij` of a reduced word are positive; every orbit
meets C; and the stabilizer of a point of C is generated by its zero-pairing
simple reflections. These are reflection-group facts, independent of any
polytope or numeric orbit enumeration. A readable primary exposition is
[Bill Casselman, *Coxeter groups*, Part I, Geometry and combinatorics,
sections “Geometry and combinatorics”, “Roots”, “The Tits cone”,
“Classification”](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~cass/coxeter/r.html).
In particular the finite-diagram theorem, not a
successful floating-point enumeration, supplies finiteness here.

For completeness, the only dominance consequence needed below has a
short proof. If p is in C and `w = s_i1 ... s_ik` is reduced, telescoping
gives

    p - w p = sum_j 2 <p,alpha_ij>
                        (s_i1 ... s_i(j-1) alpha_ij).

Thus `p - w p` is a nonnegative combination of simple roots. Consequently
`<q,p> >= <q,w p>` for every q in C. If another point z of the orbit is
in C, applying this with q=z gives `<z,p> >= ||z||^2 = ||p||^2`.
Cauchy–Schwarz forces z=p. Thus the dominant representative is unique,
including on walls.

One can also reach it constructively: if z is not in C, reflect in a
simple root with negative pairing. The scalar product with a fixed point
rho in the interior of C strictly increases. Since the orbit is finite,
this process terminates in C, hence at p. This argument also proves that
any vector can be moved into C by reflections.

For binary rings `r_i in {0,1}`, let p be the unique exact solution

    <p,alpha_i> = r_i / 2,

and put `P = conv(W p)`. Every connected component must have a ring to
obtain full dimension; otherwise delete that component and work in the
affine span. The all-zero construction is a point and is excluded from
the nondegenerate named-polytope claims. Code should reject a rings tuple
of the wrong length or with entries other than 0 and 1; its current
truth-value test (`geom.wythoff_perm`, lines 104–108) alone is weaker.

The Cholesky root basis in `geom.roots` is an orthogonal realization of
the exact G, not a new definition of G with interval endpoints substituted.
All intervals must enclose this common exact realization and the exact
linear-system solution p.

## 2. Supporting-face theorem, with proof

For `I subset S`, write `W_I = <s_i : i in I>`. For any q in C let
`I(q) = {i : <q,alpha_i> = 0}`. Then the complete maximizing vertex set
of the functional `<q, .>` on P is

    {v in W p : <q,v> = <q,p>} = W_I(q) p.                 (1)

First p maximizes by the dominance inequality above. Every generator of
W_I fixes q, so its orbit of p also maximizes. Conversely let z maximize.
Fold z into C by the negative-pairing procedure from Section 1. Each step
has increment

    <q,s_i z - z> = -2 <z,alpha_i> <q,alpha_i> >= 0.

Both the starting and final values are the maximum. Every increment is
therefore zero. At a performed step `<z,alpha_i> < 0`, so
`<q,alpha_i> = 0`; every performed reflection belongs to W_I.
The final vertex is p. Reversing these reflections gives z in W_I p.
This proves (1), without presuming a face list or a facet certificate.

Any supporting normal can be moved into C by W. All nonempty exposed
faces of P are therefore conjugates of `conv(W_I p)`. Conversely every
I is realized: solve for a q with pairing zero at I and strictly positive
at its complement. Independence of the roots gives such a q. This also
allows coincident descriptions of a face when some parabolic components
act trivially. It is a statement about sets of exact vertices, not an
assertion that every I yields a different face.

Every distinct orbit point is a vertex: its own scalar product uniquely
maximizes there, since all orbit points have the same norm. Thus the
complete orbit and the complete vertex set are identical.

## 3. Face dimensions, products, edges and recursive uniformity

Set `E_I = span{alpha_i : i in I}`. Every difference `w p - p` for
w in W_I belongs to E_I, by telescoping reflection steps. The component
of p perpendicular to E_I is fixed. After translating it away, the face
is the orbit polytope whose seed is the orthogonal projection `p_I`, with

    <p_I,alpha_i> = r_i / 2  (i in I).                    (2)

Connected components of the induced diagram have orthogonal root spans;
their groups commute and act independently. This is an orthogonal
Cartesian product of the corresponding smaller orbit polytopes. A
component with all its rings zero contributes a point.

An active connected component J contributes exactly |J| dimensions.
Indeed let D be the span of `w p_J - p_J`, w in W_J. D is W_J-invariant:
`s(w p_J-p_J) = (s w p_J-p_J) - (s p_J-p_J)` is in D. A ringed i gives
`s_i p_J-p_J = -alpha_i`, so D contains a simple root. If D contains
alpha_i and j is adjacent to i, then
`s_j alpha_i-alpha_i = -2 <alpha_i,alpha_j> alpha_j`
is in D with nonzero coefficient. Connectivity propagates through J.
Hence D contains every root of J. Therefore

    dim conv(W_I p) = sum |J| over components J of I
                              containing at least one ring.       (3)

Apply (1)–(3) to a one-dimensional face. Exactly one active component
is present, and it consists of a single ringed node i; the vertex set
is `{p,s_i p}`, up to conjugacy. Its edge vector is `-alpha_i`, of length
exactly 1. No additional, longer edges are hidden by this description,
because (1) describes every exposed face. All edges of P have length 1.

Induction on dimension now proves recursive uniformity: each face is a
product of binary-ring orbit polytopes of lower rank, again satisfying
(2). Each such face is vertex-transitive under its parabolic group.
Its proper faces satisfy the same argument. Vertices and unit segments
start the induction. In particular every two-dimensional face is a
regular polygon (a dihedral orbit) or the square product of two unit
segments. This is the usual recursive meaning of a convex uniform
polytope; it does not require all facets to be congruent.

For arbitrary convex polytopes A and B in orthogonal coordinate blocks,

    conv(V_A x V_B) = conv(V_A) x conv(V_B),
    face_(u,v)(A x B) = face_u(A) x face_v(B).              (4)

The first identity follows by multiplying convex coefficients; the
second follows by maximizing the two summands independently. Thus all
faces are products of faces, facets have one facet factor and one full
factor, and edges have one edge factor and one vertex factor. Products
of unit-edge recursively uniform polytopes are again such polytopes:
the product symmetry group is transitive and induction applies to (4).
Equal-factor coordinate swap is an additional exact isometry only when
the factors have the same exact indexed construction, not merely equal
vertex counts. These statements justify `geom.product` (line 134), the
index `i*|B|+j`, and `tcert.ProductModel`.

## 4. Facet rule and the D4/B4 equivalence in ktypes

Suppose the original system has rank n and every component is active.
A proper face has a supporting q not zero, hence `|I(q)| <= n-1`.
By (3) it is a facet exactly when I has size n-1 and every component
of I is active. Thus every facet type is obtained by deleting one node
and retaining the deletion if every remaining connected component
has a ring. Conversely every retained deletion exposes a facet.
The product decomposition in Section 3 gives its exact geometric type,
including its unit-edge scale. This proves the structural rule used by
`ktypes.facet_types` (lines 162–173); it is not inferred from vertex count.

A two-node component with edge label m gives an m-gon if one node is
ringed, and a 2m-gon if both are ringed. Reflection in the two chamber
walls makes equal unit chords; in the second case the seed is on the
angle bisector and there are twice as many vertices. A pair of
orthogonal ringed nodes instead gives the square product of segments.
The chain/reversal operations in `ktypes._part` preserve the exact Gram
matrix and the seed pairings, so give orthogonal congruences.

Here is the additional D4-to-B4 identification needed by `_part` (lines
117–123). In the B4 root system in the code, write

    beta = s_3 alpha_2 = alpha_2 + sqrt(2) alpha_3.

The four unit roots `alpha_0, alpha_1, alpha_2, beta` have D4 Gram
matrix: alpha_1 is the center, the other three roots are pairwise
orthogonal leaves, and center-leaf pairings are -1/2. In particular
`<alpha_2,beta> = 0`. Reflection s_3 fixes alpha_0, alpha_1 and
interchanges alpha_2 with beta. It normalizes the D4 subgroup H
generated by the four corresponding reflections. H together with s_3
generates B4, and every B4 word is h or h s_3 with h in H.

For a B4 seed with last ring zero, s_3 p=p, so `W(B4) p = H p`.
The pairings at the D4 leaves are `(r_0,r_2,r_2)/2` and the center
pairing is `r_1/2`. Any binary coloring of three leaves has two equal
entries y and the remaining entry x. Relabeling the leaves is an
orthogonal diagram symmetry. Consequently the D4 polytope equals
B4(x, center, y, 0), as stated by `_part`. This proves equivalence of
the whole orbit polytopes, not only a common facet signature.

For the extra triality used by `tcert.WythoffModel`, if r_0=r_2,
cyclically permuting these three leaves preserves their Gram matrix
and all seed pairings. It extends to an orthogonal map fixing p and
normalizing H, hence preserves the complete orbit. The palindromic
A3/A4/F4 flips similarly preserve the Gram matrix and pairings.
Therefore these matrices are genuine exact symmetries before Finder
tries to assign any permutation. The source-to-destination basis
formula in `WythoffModel` is `D^T (S^T)^-1`, exactly as required.

## 6. Polygon and antiprism factors

The polygon in `geom.polygon(p)` has radius `rho=1/(2 sin(pi/p))`, so
adjacent chords are exactly 1. For integer p>=3 the points are distinct,
all are exposed on their common circle, and their hull is the regular
p-gon in cyclic order. Its complete vertex set and dihedral permutations
are thus exact without any numeric deduplication.

For `geom.antiprism(n)`, assume integer n>=3 and put `theta=pi/n`.
The top angles are 2k theta, the bottom angles are (2k+1) theta, both
at radius rho, with heights +/-h/2, where

    rho = 1/(2 sin theta),
    h^2 = 1 - 2 rho^2 (1-cos theta)
        = 1 - 1/[2(1+cos theta)] > 0.

The top/bottom polygons are exposed by the vertical normal. The side
triangle with top angles 0, 2theta and bottom angle theta is exposed
by the normal whose horizontal direction is theta and whose vertical
component is `c=rho(1-cos theta)/h`. Its three support values equal
`rho(1+cos theta)/2`. Every other top vertex has strictly smaller
horizontal projection than rho cos theta, and every other bottom
vertex than rho, so they lie strictly below its support plane. Rotating
and exchanging top and bottom gives all 2n such triangles.

These are all facets, not just some exposed triangles: for a normal
with nonzero horizontal part, the maximizing subset of each regular
n-gon is one vertex or an adjacent pair. Both layers can contribute
only when their support values tie. Their edge-normal directions
differ by theta, so both layers cannot simultaneously contribute an
edge. A two-dimensional mixed supporting face therefore consists of
an edge from one layer and the intermediate vertex of the other,
exactly one of the triangles just described. A purely vertical normal
gives one of the two n-gons. This exhausts all possible facet normals.

Top/bottom edges have length 1; each side edge has squared length
`2 rho^2(1-cos theta)+h^2=1`. Every side triangle is equilateral.
Rotation by theta combined with height negation cycles all 2n
vertices, so this full hull is uniform. It also proves that the
permutations and seeds in `tcert.AntiprismModel` describe actual
symmetries and edges of the indexed exact list. No proximity argument
is needed for the direct top/bottom coordinate indexing.

## 7. Separation is a consequence, not an input to construction

For any finite set of distinct points on a common sphere, a closest
pair a,b is an edge of its convex hull. Let their distance be d>0
and their common squared norm be R^2. For any other vertex z,
minimality of d gives

    <z,a> <= R^2-d^2/2,  <z,b> <= R^2-d^2/2,

whereas

    <a+b,a> = <a+b,b> = 2R^2-d^2/2.

Thus every other vertex has strictly smaller scalar product with
a+b than a and b do. This exposes exactly their segment. If the
set has only two antipodal points the conclusion is immediate; with
other points that antipodal pair cannot be closest. Hence if every
edge is unit, the minimum distinct-point distance is 1.

The orbit, polygon, antiprism and product constructions above are
inscribed: orthogonal orbit maps preserve norm and products add
squared norms. Their unit-edge and convex-hull properties were
established without separation. Therefore their distinct vertices
are now known to have distance at least 1. The same deduction is
available for the snub and exceptional factors only after their own
complete-hull, unit-edge certificates have passed.

If interval boxes B and D contain exact vertices b and d of the same
certified model, `dist2(B,D).b < 1/4` implies `||b-d|| < 1/2` and
hence b=d. Arbitrary approximate coordinates near a vertex do not
satisfy this conclusion. Exact-vertex provenance is indispensable.

## 8. Exact indexed-list correspondence and remaining guards

The following is a sufficient contract for `PointSet` and `Finder`.
It separates mathematical claims proved above from runtime guards.

1. Every input box is finite, has the expected dimension, and encloses
   an exact vertex of one fixed, already certified construction.
   For Wythoff BFS this is proved inductively: the seed encloses p,
   and reflection in the one common exact root basis preserves the
   orbit. Interval arithmetic must preserve containment. For an
   additional symmetry the matrix must enclose the *specific exact
   symmetry* proved in Section 4, not merely a nearby orthogonal matrix.
2. For every stored or queried coordinate interval X, let m be the
   actual finite binary float returned by `vmid` for that coordinate,
   treated as its exact dyadic value. Require a certified bound
   `X subset [m-1/64,m+1/64]`. Check it by interval comparisons or
   exact endpoint arithmetic, so float conversion error is included.
   Guard the actual midpoint used in grid indexing, not only the
   mpmath interval's internal width. Dimensions here are at most 5.
3. Compute `floor(4*m)` in PointSet, or `floor(2*m)` in Finder,
   exactly for that binary float. Multiplication by these powers of
   two is exact when finite; check against overflow/nonfinite values
   or use the float's exact integer ratio. The floor integer must not
   pass through an inexact, bounded-width integer representation.
4. A returned identity requires the existing outward interval upper
   bound `<1/4` on squared distance. A failed/undecided interval test
   may reject or abort the run; it must never establish equality.
   `Finder` must raise on no match. `PointSet` must raise when a
   searched candidate has undecided possible equality, as it currently
   does. No assertion depending on this contract may be optimized away.

Here is why the grid search is complete for equality under these guards.
If two boxes contain the same exact point, their actual coordinate
midpoints differ by at most 1/32. Their cell coordinates therefore
differ by at most one, both for cells of width 1/4 and of width 1/2.
PointSet searches offsets -2,...,2; Finder searches -1,...,1. Thus an
equal stored vertex cannot be skipped. If the interval upper bound
cannot identify it, the exact common point makes the interval lower
bound on squared distance at most zero, and PointSet's undecided test
raises instead of inserting a duplicate. Finder likewise fails closed.
In particular a successful completed computation never relies on
rounding a tiny width to zero or on a heuristic statement that boxes
are “sufficiently narrow”. A full-list pairwise separation audit is
an alternative to the guard, but must precede any dependent result.

For a successfully completed Wythoff BFS:

* All inserted indices are distinct exact orbit points: induction
  supplies provenance, successful merges are sound by Section 7,
  and grid completeness prevents duplicate insertion.
* Every generator is processed on every inserted vertex. At
  termination the finite indexed set contains p and is closed under
  all simple reflections. It therefore contains the whole orbit.
  Conversely provenance shows it contains nothing else.
* The entry `perms[k][i]` is the unique exact index of `s_k(v_i)`.
  Each row is consequently a bijection and an involution, with the
  Coxeter relations. Checking bijection, involution, transitivity
  and the expected finite orbit count is useful corruption detection,
  although these checks alone do not prove coordinate containment.

This supplies exact full-list, index-by-index correspondence, not
just agreement of vertex counts or agreement of two rounded searches.
It also implies `WythoffModel` seeds are exact unit neighbors and
its unringed reflections fix vertex 0. The full stabilizer is generated
by those reflections; added proved exact symmetries may enlarge it.
The neighbor set is the stabilizer orbit of the ringed-node edge seeds:
an edge through p is some `w[p,s_i p]`; if its first endpoint is
`w s_i p=p`, replace w by w s_i. This yields an element fixing p
and the same edge. This proves completeness of `Model.nbrs()` for
these Wythoff models, as well as soundness.

`dimbound` needs only an upper bound. Every parabolic orbit lies in
`p+E_I`; applying a proved symmetry fixing p preserves that bound.
Thus `WythoffModel.flats()` and its stabilizer images are valid even
without enumerating every possible flat. Likewise a genuine Cartesian
product subset has summed affine dimension. None of these dimension
arguments assumes agreement with a floating hull routine.

Current gaps at the audited source locations are explicit: neither
`geom.PointSet.find/add` (lines 28–50) nor `tcert.Finder` (lines 98–120)
enforces the midpoint/width and finiteness guards above. Finder's
failure is conservative, but PointSet's grid omission could insert
a duplicate if input enclosures become wide; the mathematical BFS
argument requires the guard or a complete independent separation
audit. This document does not claim a classification error from that
missing guard. A guard rejection is an inconclusive replay, not a
counterexample to the classification.

The common exact-model contract for Q5 and snub factors remains their
own complete-index containment certificates. A distance `<1/2` between
an exact-coordinate box and an arbitrary approximate box cannot prove
that the latter contains that exact coordinate. Those factors must
certify actual containment at every index, or rebuild their interval
coordinates from the certified exact objects. Only then may products,
template symmetries and proximity identity inherit the proved model.

### 8a. Standalone guard for a Stage2 adapter

No change to `geom.py` or `tcert.py` is necessary to enforce this
contract. A fresh-process adapter can install subclasses of
`geom.PointSet` and `tcert.Finder` before constructing any model:

* Guard every box and every actual `vmid` coordinate before calling
  the original `PointSet.find`. Because `add` calls `self.find`, this
  guards both insertion and lookup. Record dimension 1..5 and retain
  the original fail-closed interval comparisons.
* Guard every V entry in the replacement Finder constructor and
  every y in its replacement `find`; then call the original methods.
* Treat the guard as a required acceptance condition. Restore nothing
  until all dependent templates and model permutations are built.
  Do not wrap a run with an already populated orbit/model cache.
  Use a fresh interpreter, rather than guessing which transitive
  cache dependencies to clear.

An exact implementation of the box guard needs no transcendental
arithmetic. Decode each finite mpmath endpoint tuple `(sign, man, exp,
bitcount)` to the rational `(-1)^sign * man * 2^exp`; reject special
nonfinite endpoint encodings. Decode the actual Python float m using
`m.as_integer_ratio()`. In rational arithmetic check

    m - 1/64 <= lower(X) <= upper(X) <= m + 1/64.

Also require both power-of-two scalings used in grid calculation to
stay finite; compare their exact float ratios with `2*m` and `4*m`
if desired. Never parse a rounded decimal string as the endpoint.
The wrapper does not turn arbitrary boxes into exact vertices;
provenance still comes from the construction proof and enclosing
interval operations. For Q5/snub lists it comes from their separate
actual-containment certificates.

The fresh computations that discharge the execution obligations are:

1. Freeze the source revisions and exact ordered names required by
   the S1 and Stage2 adapter, together with every named 5-model used
   by identification. Expand products into their factor constructions;
   all distinct Wythoff diagram/mask units, and every extra-symmetry
   Finder invocation, must be represented. A few control diagrams do
   not discharge the complete input contract.
2. Price at least one completed orbit unit of each materially different
   size/family, and the corresponding extra-symmetry Finder work.
   Assign the full manifest to the compute helper only after recording
   completed-unit timings and projected total work. This document has
   not started or priced those replays.
3. Run that manifest in a fresh interpreter with the required wrapper.
   For each completed unit record its name, vertex count, exact-dyadic
   maximum midpoint radius, number of guarded query boxes, generator
   row bijection/involution checks, seed indices, and permutation
   digests. Check that every intended unit completed. Derive all claims
   from the proof-backed guards, not from two numeric counts agreeing.
4. Deliberately widen a queried box and a stored box beyond the guard,
   inject a nonfinite coordinate, and corrupt a permutation entry.
   The acceptance command must reject each. A corruption in an unused
   unit is not a guard test for the manifest.

For an independently checkable finite certificate, a useful additional
representation avoids Cholesky radicals entirely. Work in the simple-root
basis over `K=Q(sqrt(2),sqrt(5))`, which contains every Gram entry in
the diagrams used here. Set `c_0=G^-1 r/2`. Simple reflection i updates
only root coefficient i:

    c'_i = c_i - 2 sum_j G_ij c_j,  c'_j=c_j (j != i).

An exact dictionary orbit gives a certificate consisting of distinct
coefficient vectors, generator transitions, the seed index, and optional
parent words. A small checker verifies all entries in K, distinctness,
every transition, and transitivity from c_0. Thus it proves the full
exact orbit without a proximity or width premise. It can certify the
actual existing interval index list by independently reconstructing
the same exact Cholesky basis at greater interval precision and
requiring **coordinatewise enclosure containment** of its exact-vector
enclosures inside each existing indexed box. If an enclosure is not
contained, refine or reject; an upper distance below 1/2 is not an
alternative containment proof. All arithmetic must preserve the common
exact Gram matrix/Cholesky convention. The finite-certificate route
still requires its own completed-unit pricing, independent check and
corruption tests; it has not been replayed here.

## 9. Status and acceptance boundaries

The supporting-face, unit-edge, recursive-uniformity, product, antiprism,
facet-type, diagram-automorphism and named Wythoff alias arguments above
are mathematical proofs. They remove the theoretical circularity in
applying separation to the binary-ring finite Coxeter constructions.

They do not certify a particular execution of interval Cholesky/solve,
a full S1/S2 input list, every exceptional/snub hull, or the final
classification. In particular, closing R2 requires the exact containment
and guarded indexed-orbit executions, the separate exceptional and
snub full-hull certificates, and their required independent checks
and deliberate corruption rejections. No change to Case B or to the
classification is proposed here.
