# Frozen protocol: WSPR anomalies on aircraft-crossed links

Written and frozen before any outcome on the sample days was computed. The
code in `code/` implements this text; where they differ, this text governs
and the difference is reported as a deviation.

## 1. Question

Do WSPR spots whose transmitter–receiver great-circle path is crossed by an
ADS-B-tracked aircraft during the transmission carry the published GDTAAA
"anomaly" flags more often than matched spots whose paths no tracked
aircraft came near?

The claim under test is the one the GDTAAA authors state: "WSPRnet links show
statistically significant anomalies when an aircraft is on the great circle
path between the transmitter and receiver" (Godfrey, in reply to a commenter, OE-FGR case
study page, 18 May 2024; [sources](SOURCES.md)). Whether WSPR can locate
aircraft, and so MH370, is an interpretation for experts and is not tested.

## 2. Data

- **WSPR:** every spot in the public wspr.live mirror of the WSPRnet
  database (`wspr.rx`: time, band, tx_sign, tx_loc, rx_sign, rx_loc,
  frequency, snr, drift), from 21:00 UTC on the day before each sample day
  to 01:00 UTC on the day after, queried hour by hour.
- **ADS-B:** ADS-B Exchange's free first-of-month `readsb-hist` snapshots
  at HH:MM:00Z for minutes 0–60 of eight one-hour blocks starting 00, 03,
  …, 21 UTC (61 snapshots per block). Each aircraft's position time is
  the snapshot's `now` minus its `seen_pos`. Positions older than 60 s are
  dropped.
- **Sample days:** the first of each month, January 2023 to December 2025
  (36 days), except 1 December 2023 (the pilot), which is replaced by
  1 December 2022. **Test slots:** the 30 two-minute WSPR slots in each block,
  so 240 per day and 8,640 in all.
- **Pilot:** 1 December 2023 (outside the sample), with 1 November 2023 as its
  placebo day, used only for mechanics: counts, timing, memory and the planted
  control's shift. Its excess was not displayed. The pilot changed the design
  before this freeze. Under the first draft (uncrossed = no aircraft within
  50 km) only 41 of 175,839 covered link-slots were uncrossed and 167,730 were
  crossed, because an airliner sweeps across nearly every path over 100 km in
  110 s. Hence the control arm in section 4, the larger sample and the
  placebo-differenced statistic in section 5.

## 3. Anomaly rule (GDTAAA, mapped before any outcome)

The source passages are quoted in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). The rule maps to code like this:

| Published rule | Code |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline from "a specific transmitterID-receiverID-frequency triple for a ±3 hour analysis window (centred on the test datum)" | triple = (tx call sign, rx call sign, band); window = the triple's spots with time in [t−3 h, t+3 h], the test spot included |
| Mean and standard deviation over the window | sample mean and sample SD (n−1), as MATLAB's `std` |
| SNR anomaly "more than 0.75 standard deviation from the mean" (current setting, 2024) | \|SNR − mean\| > 0.75 SD; SD 0 gives no anomaly |
| Frequency anomaly, same limit | \|received frequency − mean\| > 0.75 SD, in Hz |
| "green for non-zero drift"; "discard drift anomalies where there is a continual drift during this analysis timeframe" | drift ≠ 0 at the spot, and under half the window's spots have drift ≠ 0 |
| "discard candidate SNR or Frequency anomalies based on a small count"; "If the count is below five we extend up to ±3 days" | spots with fewer than 5 window spots are not scored. **Deviation:** the ±3-day extension is not implemented. It only removes sparse links from the sample and cannot create an association. |
| Mobile stations "discounted"; "detected from their call signs or the fact that the Maidenhead Grid locator changes" | spots with a call sign containing "/", a transmitter call starting 0, 1 or Q (balloon telemetry), or a call sign reported with more than one locator in the downloaded day, are dropped |
| Antenna positions from a private database | **Deviation:** 6-character Maidenhead locators (centre of the sub-square, error up to about 5 km). 4-character locators are dropped. This is why the crossing radius is 5 km, not the 1 nmi of the authors' ROC labels (1 nmi is a sensitivity run) |

**Anomaly flag A** is an SNR, frequency or drift anomaly (the union). Exact
duplicate rows are dropped first. A link-slot with several differing spots
is dropped. Links shorter than 100 km are dropped.

## 4. Crossing labels

- Aircraft tracks over a slot come from the snapshots at slot start, +60 s
  and +120 s. Positions are interpolated linearly between an aircraft's
  reports. Outside them the code dead-reckons up to 60 s from ground speed
  and track, or holds a report up to 15 s old when speed or track is missing.
  Positions are sampled at 1, 6, …, 111 s, which covers the 110.6-s
  transmission starting at second 1.
- Distance is to the **minor great-circle arc** between the locator centres
  on a 6,371-km sphere, minimised over the samples. A sign change of the
  cross-track distance inside the arc between two samples counts as
  distance 0.
- **Crossed:** an aircraft reporting barometric altitude ≥ 20,000 ft
  (FL200) comes within **5 km** of the arc.
- **Uncrossed:** no FL200+ aircraft comes within **25 km**, and no airborne
  aircraft (any altitude, or altitude unknown; aircraft reporting only
  "ground" excluded) within **5 km**. Link-slots between the two are in
  neither group.
- **Coverage mask:** a 1°×1° cell is covered in a block when at least one
  aircraft is reported in it in at least 25% of the block's snapshots. A
  link-slot is used only when every cell along its arc (sampled every
  10 km or less) is covered. This makes "uncrossed" mean "no aircraft where
  ADS-B Exchange sees aircraft".

## 5. Designs and estimator

- **Design W (same link):** controls for a crossed link-slot are the
  uncrossed slots of the same (tx, rx, band) link within ±30 minutes. This
  holds the link, station hardware and propagation regime fixed.
- **Design M (matched links):** controls are uncrossed link-slots in the
  same two-minute slot and band, sharing the transmitter or the receiver,
  not the same link, with path length within a factor 0.8–1.25. This holds
  the slot, band and one station fixed.
- For each crossed link-slot with at least one control:
  d = A − mean(A over its controls). The **excess** E is the mean of d,
  read as excess anomalies per crossed link-slot (×100 for per 100).
- E is computed three times on the same WSPR units: with the real labels,
  and with the labels from each of the two placebo days (section 6). The
  **primary statistic** is Δ = E_real − (E_placebo1 + E_placebo2)/2. Matching
  that leaves the "quiet" control link-slots systematically different from
  crossed ones gives the same excess under the placebo labels, so Δ removes
  it.
- **Uncertainty:** day-clustered bootstrap (whole sample days resampled with
  replacement, 10,000 resamples, seed 20261121), with a percentile 95%
  interval. A day is the conservative cluster: flights, receivers and
  propagation events are shared within a day.

## 6. Controls

- **Placebos:** the same labels computed from the ADS-B of the *next* and
  of the *previous* sample day at the same UTC times, cyclically over the
  sorted sample (the first day's previous is the last). This keeps the
  time-of-day traffic pattern and route geometry but breaks the physical
  coincidence. E_placebo and its interval are reported for each.
- **Planted (positive control):** in a copy of the WSPR data, 10% of the
  crossed link-slots (seed 20261121 + date) get +6 dB SNR, and the anomaly
  rule is recomputed. The test must detect this.

## 7. Outcome rule (applied in this order, `analyze.py: verdict`)

All bounds are on Δ (day-bootstrap percentile 95% interval).

1. If the planted control's Δ lower bound is not above 0 in both designs:
   **INCONCLUSIVE** (the test cannot see a planted effect).
2. If the primary Δ lower bound is above 0 in both designs: **POSITIVE**,
   with its size.
3. If the primary Δ upper bound is below 2 per 100 crossed link-slots in
   both designs: **NEGATIVE**, stated as "aircraft crossings raise the
   anomaly rate by less than 2 per 100 link-slots".
4. Otherwise **INCONCLUSIVE**.

The bound U = 2 per 100 is fixed here. At the published base rates (tens of
percent of links anomalous), a lift under 2 points gives a likelihood
ratio near 1, useless for locating an aircraft.

## 8. Reported but not decisive

These runs are all listed in `analyze.py: VARIANTS`: the 1.0-SD tier; SNR,
frequency and drift flags separately; a crossing radius of 1 nmi; crossing by
any airborne aircraft; no coverage mask; the mean |z| of SNR in place of the
flag (in z units, not per 100); a dose–response over aircraft distance bins
0–2, 2–5, 5–10 and 10–25 km against the same uncrossed controls; and
the operating point (anomaly rate among crossed and uncrossed link-slots).

## 9. Scope of the conclusion

The result is stated for: spots on WSPRnet as mirrored by wspr.live;
aircraft seen by ADS-B Exchange; paths in covered cells; the GDTAAA rule
above with 6-character locators; the 36 sample days. It says nothing about
aircraft that ADS-B does not see, about other anomaly rules, or about
methods using data beyond the WSPRnet spot fields.
