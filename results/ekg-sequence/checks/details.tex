\section{Lean formalization and verification}\label{sec:lean-notes}

The project \texttt{lean/} uses Lean 4 \cite{lean} and mathlib
\cite{mathlib}. The complete chain from the literal greedy rule
\eqref{eq:rule}, through the smoothing \eqref{eq:smooth}, to
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is machine-checked. Haver's estimates in
Proposition~\ref{prop:inputs} are \emph{imported proved theorems from Haver's
Lean sources}, recompiled in this development. They are neither
independently re-proved mathematical contributions of this paper nor
hypotheses or added axioms. The archive provides, among others,
\path{EKG/Canonical.lean}, \path{EKG/EarlyEvens.lean},
\path{EKG/Span.lean}, \path{EKG/MissingComposites.lean} and
\path{EKG/SmoothedPositions.lean}. Our new proofs of the two little-$o$
estimates, the endpoint hypotheses of the counting identity, and the final
transfer close the chain.
The elementary constants used to weaken Haver's bounds are also proved.
These are results about the actual greedy sequence, not hypotheses
added to Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

The type in the section "Literal definitions and theorem type" below is independently fixed by
\texttt{EndpointStatement} in \path{Statement.lean}.
\path{Check.lean} requires \texttt{ekg\_sharp\_all\_index} to have that type.
For the rule, \texttt{actualA\_properties}, \texttt{actualA\_gcd\_rule}
and \texttt{actualA\_gcd\_minimal} prove positivity, non-repetition,
gcd eligibility, and leastness, respectively. Thus the internal
\texttt{GreedySeq} interface is instantiated by the recursively defined
sequence, rather than left as an assumption.
The proof sources accompany this manuscript in the same deposit.

The formal $a(n)$ is the zero-based canonical sequence evaluated at
$n-1$; its value at $n=0$ is a total extension unused in the theorem.
The canonical construction proves existence, minimality, eligibility,
and distinctness for the literal rule \eqref{eq:rule}, including the
prime factor $2$. The new \texttt{smoothValue} definition excludes
$2$ in both branches. The bridge to Haver's smoothing is proved
for every $n\geq5$, using that the exceptional raw values $2,6$
occur only at indices $2,4$.

\section{Formal theorem correspondence and reproduction}
The formal theorem is \texttt{ekg\_sharp\_all\_index} in
\path{lean/SolutionEndpoint.lean}. Its statement is exactly: for every
real $\eps$, if $0<\eps$, there exists a natural $N$ with $3\leq N$
such that, for every natural $n$, $N\leq n$ implies
\eqref{eq:epsilon}, with $b(n)$ equal to
\texttt{EKG.CanonicalEndpoint.b n}. Natural numbers in that inequality
are coerced to real numbers, and the logarithm is \texttt{Real.log}.
This is the independently defined \texttt{EndpointStatement} in
\path{lean/Statement.lean}; \path{lean/Check.lean} checks the theorem
against that type. Thus the formal quantifiers are exactly those of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, including one cutoff for all later indices.

In \path{lean/EKG/EndpointDefinitions.lean}, the formal $a(n)$ is
\texttt{EKG.canonical.a (n - 1)}. The canonical sequence is the
zero-based \texttt{actualA} in \path{lean/EKG/Canonical.lean}, starting
at $1,2$; its \texttt{chooseNext} is proved in \path{lean/EKG/Basic.lean}
to select the least positive unused value sharing a prime factor with
the preceding term, equivalently having gcd greater than $1$.
This gives \eqref{eq:rule} for $n\geq1$; truncated natural subtraction
also sets $a(0)=1$, outside the theorem's range $n\geq N\geq3$.
The formal $b$ applies \texttt{smoothValue} to $a$: its two branches
select odd primes and exactly three times odd primes, respectively.
It is precisely \eqref{eq:smooth}, leaving $2$ and $6$ fixed.

The declaration has no additional sequence or analytic hypotheses.
The imported estimates are proved dependencies, not assumptions to be
supplied by the reader. \path{lean/Check.lean} guards its actual axiom
list: \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and
\texttt{Quot.sound} (propositional extensionality, classical choice,
and quotient soundness).

The standard Lake project in \path{lean/} checks Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
from the literal greedy rule, including existence, leastness and the
smoothing convention. Haver's estimates and the prime number theorem
are imported proved theorems, recompiled with the proof; their provenance
and licenses are in \path{THIRD_PARTY.md}. The build checks each proof against its independently stated type and
allows only Lean's standard logical axioms. The unrelated admitted
statements in the imported prime-number-theorem project are outside the
checked proofs. The detailed source map and axiom output are in
\path{checks/details.tex} and \path{lean/README.md}.

The Lean-checked consequences are the eventual sign, below-diagonal
asymptotic, density-one conclusion, three-line finiteness, the two
exceptional-set finiteness statements, and bounded-cycle finiteness.
The three-branch corollary and the sharper complementary count are
written deductions, not separate Lean theorems. The trusted base is
Lean's kernel and its standard logical axioms; the written deductions
are supplied in full. No human expert has reviewed this work.

The toolchain and dependencies are pinned in \path{lean/lean-toolchain}

\subsection*{Formal definitions}
The canonical sequence in the theorem is
\texttt{EKG.canonical.a (n - 1)}, defined by the zero-based
\texttt{actualA}. Its \texttt{chooseNext} is proved to select the least
positive unused integer with gcd greater than $1$ with the preceding
term. Applying \texttt{smoothValue} gives
\texttt{EKG.CanonicalEndpoint.b n}, and the theorem is compared with the
independent statement \texttt{EndpointStatement}.

\section{Literal definitions and theorem type}
For comparison with \eqref{eq:rule}, the Lean construction in
\path{EKG/Canonical.lean} is transliterated below (\texttt{Nat} denotes the
natural numbers). It is zero-based internally; \texttt{chooseNext}
selects the least positive unused eligible value, with existence and
minimality proved in \path{EKG/Basic.lean}.
\begin{verbatim}
noncomputable def actualA (n : Nat) : Nat :=
  if n = 0 then 1 else if n = 1 then 2 else
  chooseNext (fun i : Fin n => actualA i.val)
    (actualA (n-1))
termination_by n
decreasing_by all_goals omega
\end{verbatim}
The one-based interface in \path{EKG/EndpointDefinitions.lean} uses\\
\texttt{a n := EKG.canonical.a (n - 1)}. Its value at zero is an
unused total extension. Section~\ref{sec:lean} explains the checked
existence, gcd eligibility and minimality statements, not additional
assumptions on this construction.

In particular, $s(2)=2$ and $s(6)=6$. OEIS A256417 includes the prime
$2$ in both replacements \cite{oeis}; it differs from $b$ only at
indices $2,4$, where our values are $2,6$ and its values are $4,4$.
Here is the corresponding Lean definition, transliterated from
\path{EKG/EndpointDefinitions.lean}, using ASCII type names:
\begin{verbatim}
def smoothValue (m : Nat) : Nat :=
  if And m.Prime (Not (m = 2)) then 2 * m
  else if And (m / 3).Prime
      (And (Not (m / 3 = 2)) (m = 3 * (m / 3))) then
    2 * (m / 3)
  else m
noncomputable def b (n : Nat) : Nat := smoothValue (a n)
\end{verbatim}
Here \texttt{And} and \texttt{Not} are Lean's logical constructors;
the distributed source uses the usual symbolic notation. All logarithms are natural.

The Lean type, transliterated using \texttt{abs}, \texttt{And} and \texttt{Exists}
for the usual logical notation and opening \texttt{EKG.CanonicalEndpoint},
is
\begin{verbatim}
theorem ekg_sharp_all_index :
  forall eps : Real, 0 < eps ->
    Exists (fun N : Nat => And (3 <= N)
      (forall n : Nat, N <= n ->
        abs ((b n : Real) - (n : Real) -
          (n : Real) / (3 * Real.log (n : Real))) <=
        eps * ((n : Real) / Real.log (n : Real))))
\end{verbatim}
The theorem has no sequence-estimate hypotheses. The separate statement
in \path{Statement.lean} and its type check in \path{Check.lean} fix
exactly these quantifiers and casts.


\section{Numerical generation and validation}
The included \path{data/ekg-table.cpp} generates exact integer
terms using a bitmap and one least-unused-multiple cursor per prime,
as in the method of LRS Section 3. Every eligible value is a multiple
of a prime divisor of the preceding term, so the minimum of these
cursors gives the next value. The storage bound is checked at every
selection; exhausting it aborts rather than truncating the candidate
search. A separate direct gcd scan validates the first $4096$ terms
and rejects an altered prefix. All first $4096$ smoothed values are
also checked by trial division. The table file records $18$ selected
indices through $5\cdot10^8$, with integer digests in
\path{data/ekg-table-summary.json}.

The ratios were certified by bounding $\log n$ with exact
rational sums for $2\operatorname{atanh}z$. After reducing the argument
to $[1,2]$, one uses $60$ positive terms with $0\leq z\leq1/3$ and tail
at most $2z^{121}/(121(1-z^2))$. This certifies an absolute error
at most $10^{-17}$ in the recorded ratios and correct rounding to
nine decimal places in the original display,
Table~\ref{tab:ratio-original}. The current paper rounds the same
values to four decimal places in Table~\ref{tab:ratio}.
