# Protocol addendum: a second, unseen sample of 36 dates

Written and committed on November 22, 2026, on the owner's instruction,
before any data for the new dates was downloaded or labeled. It adds a
replication sample and a pooled analysis. Everything in
[PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) stays as frozen; the code in `code/` runs
unchanged, except that the date list comes from `code/days_new.py` instead
of `code/days.py`.

## Why

On the first 36 dates the frozen outcome is NEGATIVE, but leaving out one
date (2025-12-01) raises design W's upper limit to 2.01 (NOTE.md, Sensitivity
runs). The owner asked for a fresh set of dates the analysis has never seen,
doubling the sample, analyzed with the frozen designs, bar, placebos and
planted control.

## 1. New dates

ADS-B Exchange publishes its free `readsb-hist` samples only for the first
day of each month. On November 22, 2026 they exist from July 1, 2016 to
November 1, 2026, except October 1, 2017 (checked by requesting each month's
00:00:00Z snapshot). The dates used before are the 36 sample dates and the
pilot date, December 1, 2023 (PROTOCOL.md section 2).

The new sample is fixed by one rule: **the 36 most recent first-of-month
dates not used before, counting back from November 1, 2026.** That is
January 1 to November 1, 2026 (11 dates) and November 1, 2020 to
November 1, 2022 (25 dates). `code/days_new.py` lists them.

If a new date's downloads are incomplete (any of the 488 snapshots missing,
or any WSPR hour failing after the fetcher's retries), it is reported and
replaced by the next earlier unused first-of-month date, in order. No date is
replaced for any other reason.

## 2. Analysis

- **Units:** `code/fetch.py` and `code/prep.py`, unchanged, on each new date.
- **Placebos:** the next and previous date of the *new* sample, cyclically
  over the sorted new sample (PROTOCOL.md section 5, applied to the new
  list). The first 36 dates keep their recorded units and placebos.
- **New dates alone:** `code/analyze.py` on the 36 new units files, with
  its frozen designs, statistic, day bootstrap (10,000 draws, seed
  20261121), variants, planted control and decision rule.
- **Pooled:** `code/analyze.py` on all 72 units files (old and new), with
  the same settings. Days are the resampling units, as before.
- **Dependence on single dates:** `code/sens.py` on the 72 files (leave one
  date out, cyclic block bootstraps of 2, 3, 4 and 6 dates over the
  date-sorted pooled sample, and its matching variants), and on the 36 new
  files.
- **Spot check:** `code/check.py` on 20 random units of three new dates
  drawn with `random.Random(20261122)`; any mismatch is reported and
  traced before the outcome is used.

## 3. Decision, fixed now

The pooled analysis decides; the new dates alone are reported beside it
and do not change the reading.

- **Robust negative:** the pooled verdict under the frozen rule is NEGATIVE
  (both pooled upper limits below 2 per 100, both planted lower limits above
  0), **and** it stays NEGATIVE in each of the 72 single-date deletions and
  at each block length 2, 3, 4 and 6. The paper then states the negative as
  robust to these checks.
- **Otherwise:** the claim becomes "inconclusive at this sensitivity",
  stated plainly, with the pooled estimates and intervals, and the pooled
  verdict under the frozen rule reported as it falls. A pooled POSITIVE
  under the frozen rule is reported as POSITIVE.

## 4. Estimate, written before the run

From the first run's receipts (NOTE.md, Attempts): fetch about 42 CPU-s
per date, prep about 0.31 CPU-hours per date at a peak of 8.2–8.5 GB, the
36-date analysis 0.67 CPU-hours at 6.1 GB. For the new sample: fetch 0.4,
prep 11.2, analysis of the new dates 0.7, pooled analysis about 1.4 (about
12 GB peak, under 80% of the 17 GB sandbox), `sens.py` on 36 and 72 dates
about 1.5, checks 0.2: about 15.4 CPU-hours, and at most 23 with a factor
of 1.5. Data: about 0.5 GB per date after reduction, about 18 GB in all,
in scratch. Two prep workers fit in memory, so the wall time is about
6 to 8 hours. A timed piece of one new date is run before the batch.

## 5. Timed piece and revised estimate (added before the batch)

One new date drawn with `random.Random(20261122)`, 2026-08-01, with its
placebo dates: three fetches took 50 to 54 CPU-s each (3.5 to 3.8 minutes
wall, at most 0.17 GB); `prep.py` took 1,593 CPU-s (0.44 CPU-hours, 27
minutes wall) at a peak of 8.29 GB, on 9.3 million WSPR rows. Priced at that
piece for every date, prep is 16 CPU-hours and the batch about 19, at most 28
with a factor of 1.5. Two 8.3 GB workers would pass 80% of the lead's 16 GB
sandbox, so the batch runs `run-new.sh` unchanged on a 96 GB, 12-core
compute sandbox with 9 prep workers and 3 downloads at a time.

## 6. Replacements (after the downloads, before any labeling)

The downloads found missing ADS-B snapshots on two new dates: 2020-12-01
(1 of 488, 00:05) and 2021-01-01 (4 of 488: 18:00, 18:11, 18:12, 19:00). By
section 1 each is replaced by the next earlier unused first-of-month date
with every snapshot present. 2020-10-01 has all 488. 2020-09-01 lacks two
(21:17 and 21:18), so the second replacement is 2020-08-01, which has all
488 (checked by requesting each snapshot on November 22, 2026). The new
sample is now 2020-08-01, 2020-10-01, 2020-11-01, 2021-02-01 to
2022-11-01, and 2026-01-01 to 2026-11-01. No units file or outcome had been
computed for any new date except the timed piece's 2026-08-01 labels.
